## Meaning

If ~ just  If ~ only

if you just have a map, you

## Notes

### 1 — dc

さえ is often used in a pattern like:<br/>
<br/>
noun-sae　verb-ba = result<br/>
noun-only verb-if = result<br/>
<br/>
ひま&lt;b&gt;さえ&lt;/b&gt;　あれ&lt;b&gt;ば&lt;/b&gt;　幸せです<br/>
free time &lt;b&gt;only&lt;/b&gt; &lt;b&gt;if&lt;/b&gt; I had = I would be happy<br/>
if only i had some free time, i would be happy<br/>
<br/>
薬&lt;b&gt;さえ&lt;/b&gt;　飲め&lt;b&gt;ば&lt;/b&gt;、直ります<br/>
medicine &lt;b&gt;only&lt;/b&gt; &lt;b&gt;if&lt;/b&gt; you drink = get better<br/>
if you just take your medicine, you will get better

### 2 — deeana

ば - most commonly used<br/>
たら - can be used, but is less common<br/>
なら - only in limited contexts<br/>
*と　- unacceptable<br/>
<br/>
ex：君さえ承知してくれたら・(してくれる）なら、今すぐこのプロジェクトを始めるつもりだ。<br/>
As long as you agree (to our plan), we are planning to begin this project right away

## Discussion

### 1 — bamboo4

In the example, 何の迷いもなく到着します does not make sense. It should be 迷わないで到着できたのに(we would have gotten to our destination without going astray). 迷いもなく is okay, but normally it is used to describe abstruct mental confusion rather than physical or concrete one.
