## Meaning

please

Please give me,that

## Notes

### 1 — ramu

<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> is an auxiliary verb which indicates a polite request: "please do"<br/>
<br/>
1) kudasai is a polite imperative form of <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>, the honorofic version of <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> 'give'(me) and is used as an auxiliary verb with the te-form of verbs.<br/>
　ゆっくり飲んでください。<br/>
　please drink slowly/at your own pace<br/>
<br/>
2) The negtative question form <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> makes a request more polite　<br/>
　あした８時に来てくださいませんか。<br/>
　Won't you come tomorrow at 8 O'Clock?<br/>
<br/>
3) <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> emphasizes the speakers request and makes it more polite.<br/>
　どうぞたくさん食べてください。<br/>
　Go ahead please, and eat as much as you like<br/>
<br/>
4) In very informal speech, kudasai may drop(this form of request is often used by female speakers)<br/>
　はやく言って。<br/>
　go on say it (please)<br/>
<br/>
　まだ帰らないで。<br/>
　dont come back yet (please)<br/>
<br/>
5) <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>, the imperative form of kureru, can also be used in place of kudasai in informal male speech.[Vte/Vneg nai de] kure is the least polite request form.<br/>
　いっしょに来てくれ。<br/>
　can you come together (for me?)<br/>
  (=can you let me go with you?)<br/>
<br/>
　うちへ帰らないでくれ。<br/>
　don't come back to our place (OK?)

## Discussion

### 1 — Amatuka

Note : The object marker を is <b>said</b> 'o' but may be <b>written</b> as either 'wo' or 'o' depending on romaji system used.

### 2 — Amatuka

ください can be used in two ways.<br/>
1. To ask for an object をください ( wo kudasai )<br/>
2. To ask for an action verb-te ください (v-te kudasai).

### 3 — Amatuka

Note みせる (miseru) is the <b>dictionary</b> form of the verb meaning 'to show'.<br/>
みせて (misete) is the <b>te</b> form of the verb meaning 'to show'.

### 14 — KyleGoetz

I went through the examples and replaced 下 with くだ. Japanese manuals of style say to use the kanji with NOUNを下さい, contrasted with the TE-form VERBてください, in which you do not use the kanji.<br/>
<br/>
It's not technically incorrect, but it's preferred, similar to how in English spelling out zero through ten is preferred, but using numbers for values greater than ten is preferred.
