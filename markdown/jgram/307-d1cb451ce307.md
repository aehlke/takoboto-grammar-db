the colder it is, the more delicious sake can be made

## Discussion

### 1 — bamboo4

In the second example, 閉まらないほど would be "cannot be closed" instead of opened.<br/>
In the third example, "can be made" assumes possibility of human intervention, whereas 出来る denies that. So, the English should be "can become" instead of "can be made."

### 2 — gtunak85

ex. 666 and 1211 seem different from 263.<br/>
The first two have the effect coming before ほど.. can not speakほどangry. can not openほどcrowded.<br/>
Example 263 seems backwards, but is similar to the explanation for 〜ば〜ほど.<br/>
Could someone explain the first two examples, please?

### 3 — gtunak85

To add to that, the first two examples, ほど has the effect of "such that."<br/>
Such that he could not speak, he was angry.<br/>
Such that the door could not be closed, it was crowded.<br/>
Does that soudn right?

### 4 — bamboo4

I think it is smoother if the English versions of the first two examples read:<br/>
He was so angry that he could hardly speak.<br/>
It was so crowded that the door cluld not be closed.<br/>
