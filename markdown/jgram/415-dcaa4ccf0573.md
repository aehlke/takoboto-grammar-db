## Meaning

to; for 〜から見た

The problem for Tarou is that he doesn't understand English.

## Notes

### 1 — rubyhatchet

＊にとっての is simply the prenominal form of にとって. It doesn't have some mystical separate meaning from にとって, but as a compound particle, にとって(の) does have different connotations from には, which does *not* have a prenominal form.<br/>
<br/>
For example:<br/>
太郎にとっての問題は... (The problem for Tarou is...)<br/>
〜にとってのいい機会 （A good opportunity for〜)<br/>
その歌手は私にとってのアイドルの一人だ。 (That singer is an idol to me.)<br/>
<br/>
＊にとって(の) VS には: Because of the particle は, には gives a sense of contrast in some contexts while にとって(の) does not.<br/>
FOR EXAMPLE: これは様々[には/にとって]無視できない問題だ。<br/>
In the above example, the sense with には can imply that not for others but for us, or at least for us, this is a problem which cannot be ignored. The sentence with にとって does not have this implication.<br/>
<br/>
FORMATION:<br/>
Ｎ1 + にとっての + Ｎ2
