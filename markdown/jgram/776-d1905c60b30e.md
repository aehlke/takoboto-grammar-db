### 10 — televangelist

A few notes from the trenches are in order. :)<br/>
<br/>
Out here in Nagoya, you will hear men use "wa" *very* rarely.  It's still an oddity such that as someone learning Japanese, you're best off just leaving it alone.<br/>
<br/>
Previous posters are right - it's not heard much from women anymore either.  When you do, it's mostly middle-aged women who are affecting an air of sophistication; but much older than middle-aged, and "kashira" seems much more prevalent.<br/>
<br/>
The good news for a Japanese-learner is that 'wa' is simple:  You understand that it's just flavorful emphasis, it's at the end of a sentence so it won't throw you off, and then you never ever use it and you'll be fine.<br/>
<br/>
I don't think Miki is fully correct, though, about あれ・あら.  At the very least, it varies based on dialect and is not a universal thing.  I hear a lot of elderly men who speak Nagoya-ben and Mikawa-ben use "Ara" rather than "are" - either as a drawn-out "Araaaa.." or a quick repetition of "Ara-ara-ara" naturally depending on circumstance.  It's not a strong "ra" sound, though, maybe halfway between "ara" and "are"?  Just a subtlety thing.  If you split the difference between the two, you should be fine. ;)<br/>
<br/>

### 11 — KWhazit

Part of the confusion here is that there isn't just a single use for わ. If I may borrow from an online dictionary (dictionary.goo.ne.jp)...<br/>
The two that apply here (with my translations added) are:<br/>
<br/>
(1)（女性用語として）話し手の主張や決意を、表現をやわらげて軽く言い表す。 - (as feminine language) Softens presentation and expresses the speaker's assertion, determination, etc., mildly.<br/>
ex: 「おもしろいわね」 - "How interesting"<br/>
(2)軽い詠嘆や驚きなどの気持ちを表す。 - Expresses mild feelings of admiration, surprise, etc.<br/>
ex: 「これは驚いたわ」 - "This's a surprise"<br/>
<br/>
Note that only the first is marked as feminine.<br/>
<br/>
And to partially answer chaser48's question, 〜よ (see <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>) is a gender-neutral way to add emphasis,  though it's not quite equivalent, and may even appear alongside わ as in <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=wa#">#</a></b>.

### 12 — mdchachi

There *is* a gender-neutral or masculine 〜わ.  I still remember the first time I heard it about 20 years ago since I was so shocked to hear a guy use a supposedly feminine ending-wa. But this is actually not feminine at all.  As I encountered it, it's used when taking action particularly after making a decision to take action.  Like そうするわ which kind of has the meaning of そうしてみます.
