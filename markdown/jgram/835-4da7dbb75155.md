## Meaning

because (of a negative thing); simply because; simply on account of

he was fired just for talking back

## Notes

### 1 — dc

because of ... always indicates a negative outcome.

### 2 — LR

A conjunction indicating that a single factor causes a negative situation

### 3 — LR

<b>BE CAREFUL</b> not to confuse ばかりに with とばかりに.<br/>
<strong>ばかりに</strong> is for indicating a single factor that causes a negative situation and is formed <br/>
{VERB-past-tense / いadj. / なadj.stem / NOUN} +ばかりに<br/>
<strong>とばかりに</strong> indicates that s.o does s.t in such a way that s.o. is convinced that something is the case - "思い立ったが吉日とばかりにエアロビクスを始めました。<br/>
I began aerobics believing that the day you think of something is an auspicious day to start it." and is formed<br/>
{direct quotation / indirect quotation / noun} + とばかりに<br/>
See <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>

### 4 — bamboo4

The first ばかりに and the second one do not mean the same thing. ばかりに in the first example means "simply because" or "simply for the reason" suggesting some ad hoc intervention by a fortuitous circumstances, whereas ばかりに in the seond example results from a logical cause and effect and there is no element of luck there.

〜ばかりに　can be said as "just because of 〜、.....(followed by negative consequences)"<br/>

### 12 — bamboo4

Generally, ばかり has the connotation of limiting something and not going beyond. ばかりに therefore means "because so limited," and ばかりで means "not going beyond that limit." <br/>
Accordingly, 物価は上がるばかりで would mean "since the commodity prices only go up..."
