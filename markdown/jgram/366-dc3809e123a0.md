## Meaning

no need to 〜しなくても　いい

There's no need to be insulting, you know\!

## Discussion

### 1 — Kaens

I'd rather define this as (verb/inf.)ことはない, the meaning being the simple verb-&gt;gerund conversion + negation and thus referring to <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>

### 2 — Miki

<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=koto%20ha%20nai#">#</a></b> 文字化け？　I guess 時間は十分あるから急ぐことはない。　十分 here can be read as じゅうぶんenough or じゅっぷん 10 minutes.

### 3 — Cleber

Is it [必要とする] the opposite expression to  [ことはない ]??

### 4 — karamu san

I have also seen the expression: (verb/inf.)必要はない used to mean the same thing. Is there a difference nuance between these, or one preferred over another?

### 5 — tigert

Is this right? Shouldn`t it be 〜ことではない<br/>
〜ことはない　means that thing isn`t or doesn`t exist but ではない means its not that kind of thing.<br/>
<br/>
I don`t think I have heard a native speaker use ことはない<br/>
<br/>
<br/>
jyubun　can also mean enough or sufficent. so <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=koto%20ha%20nai#">#</a></b> could be translated as<br/>
<br/>
because there is sufficent time, there is no need to hurry.<br/>
<br/>

### 6 — corbettgreg

I hear this grammar pattern used by native speakers all the time. I'd say it's the number one way to say there's no need/it's not necessary.

### 7 — herinapotter

「必要はない」and「ことはない」have the same meaning.
