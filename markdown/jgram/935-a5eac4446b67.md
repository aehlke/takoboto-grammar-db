## Meaning

is (inanimate)

There is a television in my kitchen.

## Notes

### 1 — dc

<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> is used for inanimate things, ie objects rather than animals or people.<br/>
<br/>
see also <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> いる the equivalent for living things.<br/>
<br/>
take's が particle.

### 2 — Rinji\_HalfElf

<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> is one of three common verbs with the equivalent of English verb "to be".<br/>
<br/>
Usage is roughly equivalent to "is" or "Exists" for inanimate things.<br/>
Living things are generally excluded.<br/>
For instance, a cat would use the verb <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> as it has intelligence, and an insect would use <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>. But living things such as plants use <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>.<br/>
<br/>
<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> is stative (non-action) verb. To express location, the particle <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>/に is used. Otherwise <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>/が　is used (if you can find some exceptions to this rule, please let us know!).<br/>
<br/>
Example:<br/>
本はテエブルの上にあります。　ほんはテエブルのうえにあります。<br/>
(The) book is on table's top. Hon ha teeburu no ue ni arimasu. <br/>
<br/>
<b>*</b> "to have" is also expressed often using <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>. The thing that is being possession in this construction, uses the particle <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>/が.<br/>
<br/>
Example:<br/>
お金がありますか？　おかねがありますか？<br/>
(as for you) does money exist? Okane ga arimasu ka?<br/>
<br/>
<b>Other notes:</b><br/>
<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> is generally written in kana alone, instead of using kanji.<br/>
<br/>
Forgetting to use が and using plain form <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> might make your sound rude and like a yakuza member. Dropping を has a similar effect.

## Discussion

### 1 — Amatuka

I'll have to look into it some time.

### 2 — Amatuka

ている <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> can't be used with the verbs of existance (e.g. ある <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> and いる <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>)

### 3 — bamboo4

在る indicates the physical existence of something, while 有る could be either possession or existence of some state or event. In either case, it is normal to use ある without making fine distinction.

### 4 — Eeker

To be anal, I think the translation of 丘の上に家が在り（あり）ます is slightly off... In English we do not generally use "located" when the article is indefinite, i.e. when you use "a" or "an".  I believe a more accurate translation would be "There is a house on the hill".

### 5 — bamboo4

I think Eeker is correct.

### 6 — Saralynne

I've seen in some examples that ある is used to mean "to have"... and I know there is another word for "to hold" or "to carry", but is this how you say you have something?
