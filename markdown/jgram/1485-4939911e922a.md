## Meaning

just (just recently) / nothing but

I just bought it / you do nothing but play around

## Notes

### 1 — han-na\_bae

ta form + bakari

### 2 — duncane

bakari is a "completed fullness", for instance that's all you got because with that you didn't need any more.<br/>
dake means that's all you got, but more would be better/ok/possible/,,,

### 3 — KWhazit

I think this needs to be split into two separate entries.<br/>
<br/>
ばかり (after a past-tense verb) in [<b><a href="http://jgram.org:80/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=bakari-2#5018">#5018</a></b>], [<b><a href="http://jgram.org:80/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=bakari-2#5027">#5027</a></b>], [<b><a href="http://jgram.org:80/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=bakari-2#5034">#5034</a></b>], and others, means that something has only just happened<br/>
<br/>
ばかり (after a noun, -te form verb, etc.) in many of the other examples ([<b><a href="http://jgram.org:80/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=bakari-2#5021">#5021</a></b>], [<b><a href="http://jgram.org:80/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=bakari-2#5028">#5028</a></b>], [<b><a href="http://jgram.org:80/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=bakari-2#5031">#5031</a></b>], etc.) is more of a "nothing but", "all it ever does is", or "all that's there is" feeling.

### 5 — drdave

I second KWhazit's comment: there are most definitely two separate entries here. <br/>
Also need to beware: depending on meaning ("just happened"/"nothing but"), placement is different:<br/>
<br/>
食べたばかり -&gt; I just ate (moments ago).<br/>
ばかり食べた -&gt; All I/we/etc did was eating (and nothing else).<br/>
<br/>
Furthermore, I believe pronunciation/spelling: ばっかり is also valid (very often heard in speech).
