## Meaning

Talking about relative location of an object ある物について立地を説明するために

My hat is in your car

## Notes

### 1 — tigert

Location is specified with the combination of a location noun and/or relative place word (上、下、中、外、後ろ、前、横、そば）and the location marker に､and one of the two existance verbs ある or いる<br/>
<br/>
ある　is used when talking about inanimate objects or states of being. while いる　is used for animate objects.<br/>
<br/>
My dog is in your car<br/>
わたしの犬はあなたの車にいます｡<br/>
My hat is in your car<br/>
わたしのぼうしはあなたの車にあります｡<br/>
<br/>
If you want to specify a relative location IE in something, next to something, on something. you combine the　location noun with the relative location word using the posessive particle　の<br/>
<br/>
On the desk<br/>
つくえの上<br/>
In the bag<br/>
かばんの中<br/>
by the tv<br/>
テレビの横<br/>
behind the car<br/>
車の後ろ<br/>
infront of the computer<br/>
パャRンの前<br/>
<br/>
becareful to include the location particle に at the end of the relative location statement.<br/>
で can also be used for location, but not in combination with the いる　and ある verbs.<br/>
<br/>

According to my Japanese friend they never use "の中に" when you are picking items out of a list (like picking your favorite).   They say, "スポーツの中で、どちらが一番好き？　I think that "の中に" translates more to "within" and "の中で”is "in or out of".  So the sentence 箱の中に、なにがあるの？ is correct because it can be translated as "What is there within the box?"<br/>

### 5 — bsloh

の中に is to indicate object location, for example スパートの中に本屋があります。(Bookshop is in the supermarket.)<br/>
<br/>
の中で is exlpain as "among the category", for example 果物の中で、どちらが一番すきですか。(Among the fruits,what is your favourite?)

I hope you don`t mind but I added some grammar to your grammar note. As for に and で they are used in much the same way, but they are not interchangable. に　is used for static states of being, while で is used for dynamic states or action. if your verb is いる､ある　use に　if its anything else で would be a good bet.
