## Meaning

in order to,  to

It takes time to learn Japanese

## Notes

### 1 — tenkamuteki82

-my notes-<br/>
のに is different from ので<br/>
<br/>
EX)From Jgram,Jlpt3<br/>
日本語を覚えるのに時間がかかります<br/>
<br/>
のにis used the same exact way as ので, but のに means "despite" or "in order to".<br/>
<br/>
Another Example:(From tai kims japanese guide)<br/>
毎日運動したのに、全然痩せなかった。 <br/>
<br/>
-end my notes-

### 2 — agnestan

I believe the example from Tae Kim's guide above refers to のに in the sense of "despite"/"although", not "in order to" as in this のに entry.

## Discussion

### 1 — Amatuka

Form is <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> + [nominaliser の] + に

### 2 — Amatuka

I'm not that certain of what I typed in the 'see also' now - although the two are quite different usages.

### 3 — Norgus

ex <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=noni-2#">#</a></b> has furigana for 何時 as どこ, which is simply not true and should be read なんじ, which has nothing to do with place but is asking 'what time'.<br/>
<br/>
Doko is normally written in just hiragana anyway.<br/>
<br/>
reguarding the first example <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=noni-2#">#</a></b> 日本語を覚えるのに時間がかかります<br/>
Is there an easy way to tell that it wasn't intended to mean 'despite that I am going to remember Japanese, it will take time' ?<br/>
(and wouldn't the verb 習う(ならう) 'to learn' fit this best?)

### 4 — KyleGoetz

@Norgus, it does not sound like the "although" のに because when using the form ＡのにＢ to show a contrast, A and B must be contrastive. "I am going to remember Japanese" and "It will take time" are not really that contrastive. It just doesn't sound like that. That's all I can offer on the topic.
