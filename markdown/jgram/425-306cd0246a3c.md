## Meaning

probably won't

you probably won't die from this illness

## Notes

### 1 — rubyhatchet

This is an auxiliary which expresses the negative volition or conjecture of the speaker. It is basically a written form, used in formal writing.<br/>
<br/>
For Gr. 2 verbs, Ｖmasu is occasionally used instead of Ｖinf・nonpast. For irregular verbs, すまい and こまい are sometimes used instead of するまいand くるまい, respectively. See examples <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=mai-2#">#</a></b>, <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=mai-2#">#</a></b>, and <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=mai-2#">#</a></b>.<br/>
<br/>
まい does not have a past form. The past tense is expressed by other verbs. See examples <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=mai-2#">#</a></b> and <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=mai-2#">#</a></b>.<br/>
<br/>
The volition of someone other than the speaker can be expressed by まい, as in example <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=mai-2#">#</a></b>. Note that と思う is used to express the volition of someone other than the speaker. Thus, example <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=mai-2#">#</a></b> expresses the conjecture of the speaker rather than the volition of Keiko.<br/>
<br/>
Ｖinf・nonpastまいとする expresses the idea of "try not to Ｖ." See example <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=mai-2#">#</a></b>. When this structure is used in a subordinate clause, する is often omitted, as in example <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=mai-2#">#</a></b>.<br/>
<br/>
ＶvolitionalかＶinf・nonpastまいか expresses the idea "whether (I) will Ｖ or not," as in example <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=mai-2#">#</a></b>.

### 2 — rubyhatchet

Ｖnonpastまい<br/>
1. 行くまい (〜will not go; probably will not go)<br/>
2. 教えるまい (〜will not teach; probably will not teach)<br/>
<br/>
Adj(i)stemくはあるまい<br/>
1. 高くはあるまい (〜is probably not expensive)<br/>
<br/>
Adj(na)stem/Nounであるまい<br/>
1. 静かではあるまい (〜is probably not quiet)<br/>
2. 先生ではあるまい (〜is probably not a teacher)

## Discussion

### 1 — Amatuka

This usage is now generally replaced by ないだろう

### 2 — Amatuka

Commonly used with あるまい

### 3 — Amatuka

Hmm, my example might be 'mai-3' : expression of a negative that is natural or appropriate.

### 4 — Miki

As for the first example, we don't say 死ぬまい for the third person. We'd say 病気で死ぬことはあるまいと思うが、…。<br/>
When you talk about yourself, you'd say この病気で死ぬまい、絶対生きてやる。

### 6 — Miki

まぬかれる is a dictonary form. It is also pronounced as まぬがれる. 免れる means 逃れる. <br/>
まぬがれ + まい (neg) &gt;&gt;&gt; You will not be able to escape from being punished this time.<br/>
