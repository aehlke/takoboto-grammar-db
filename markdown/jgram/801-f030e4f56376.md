## Meaning

started but not finished

the half-drunk thing in the fridge

## Notes

### 1 — dc

&lt;b&gt;~kakeru&lt;/b&gt; is for verbs:<br/>
言いかけ　（いいかけ）<br/>
In the middle of saying something<br/>
<br/>
本を読みかけて、寝てしまいました<br/>
I fell asleep with the book half read.<br/>
<br/>
&lt;b&gt;~kakeno&lt;/b&gt; is a modifier for nouns (eg. tabekake no ringo)<br/>
食べかけのりんご<br/>
A half-eaten apple<br/>
<br/>
&lt;b&gt;~kake da&lt;/b&gt; comes at the end of a sentence<br/>
where だ is derived from です<br/>
書きかけだ<br/>
it is in the middle of being written<br/>

### 10 — dc

### 11 — your name

食べかけの is simply the noun form of 食べかける with の acting as a modifier.  I think there is no difference in meaning between '食べかけたケーキ' and '食べかけのケーキ' (although the latter is much more common).

### 12 — dc

as someone else pointed out the かけた in 話しかけた　is nothing to do with this grammar, it's a set phrase <br/>
彼は私に話しかけた。<br/>
"he started to talk to me"
