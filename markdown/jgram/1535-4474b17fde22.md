## Meaning

How to conjugate regular adjectives ending in "i". い形容詞の変化し方

(I-adjective) + (copula)

## Notes

### 1 — Rushyne

This is the conjugation of an I-adjective with Plain and Polite form and timing tenses (present and past)

### 2 — Rushyne

The plain form of an I-adjective does not use the da coupula it leaves it blank.

## Discussion

### 1 — KyleGoetz

I changed the information about this entry, because it is not just about the adjective "chikai", but rather about the method of conjugating i-adjectives as a whole. I also fixed a typo.<br/>
<br/>
The spacing of the romanized words need to be fixed in the example sentences -- the current formatting muddies the logic behind the grammar. Instead of "chika kunakatta", it should be "chikaku nakatta", because the grammatical construct behind the form is the adverbial form of "chikai" ("chikaku") plus the plain negative past form of "aru" ("nakatta").<br/>
<br/>
Of course, I believe it is better to have no space, favoring<br/>
chikai, chikakunai, chikakatta, chikakunakatta<br/>
chikai desu, chikakunai desu (or chikaku arimasen), chikakatta desu, chikakunakatta desu (or chikaku arimasen deshita). However, if you feel the need to put spaces, they more logically should be as follows:<br/>
chikai, chikaku nai, chikakatta, chikaku nakatta<br/>
chikai desu, chikaku nai desu (or chikaku arimasen), chikakatta desu, chikaku nakatta desu (or chikaku arimasen deshita).<br/>
