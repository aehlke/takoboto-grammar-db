## Meaning

For japanese steak, this is pretty tasty.

## Notes

### 1 — dc

<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> always follows a noun, whereas <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> can be used after a noun or adjective<br/>
&lt;font color=red&gt;<br/>
noun + toshite ha<br/>
noun + no wari ni<br/>
&lt;/font&gt;<br/>
先生としては、若いです。<br/>
先生&lt;b&gt;の&lt;/b&gt;割りに、若いです<br/>
for a teacher, he is young<br/>
<br/>
&lt;font color=red&gt;<br/>
adj +  warini<br/>
&lt;/font&gt;<br/>
OK○　高い割りに、不味かった<br/>
for the expensive price, it wasnt very tasty<br/>
<br/>
NG×　高いとしては、不味かった　<br/>
cannot use toshiteha after adjective<br/>

### 2 — gtunak85

toshiteha and <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> imply that something is odd or unexpected. <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> and <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> do not really have such an implication.<br/>
<br/>
Comparison:<br/>
敏之としては、数学が上手です。<br/>
For Toshiyuki, he is good at math.<br/>
としては implies, perhaps, that Toshiyuki is usually bad at any academics, so it is odd that he is good at math. So the meaning might be, "for someone as stupid as Toshiyuki, it's amazing that he's actually good at math."<br/>
<br/>
敏之にとって、数学が上手です。<br/>
For Toshiyuki, he is good at math.<br/>
にとって implies that Toshiyuki is normally good at math. So the meaning might be, "Toshiyuki is good at math, but we kinda all knew that already."

### 3 — rubyhatchet

＊This is a compound particle which indicates a standard for comparisons.<br/>
<br/>
FORMATION:<br/>
Ｎ + としては<br/>
<br/>
＊Be careful! として (<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>) has a different connotation. See that entry for more.
