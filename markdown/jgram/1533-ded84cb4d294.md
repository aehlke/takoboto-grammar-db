## Meaning

is, am, are

(NA-adjective) + (stem) + desu

## Notes

### 1 — pingpongpaddle

desu comes at the end of the sentence

### 2 — pingpongpaddle

If conversing with friends, family, etc, desu is not necessary.<br/>
これは私のペン。<br/>
desu is only necessary when talking to adults, teachers, etc...

### 3 — talon\_tearine

Desu is placed at the end of the sentence after:<br/>
1) Nouns<br/>
2) Adjectives (I-adj remain the same whereas there is no 'na' after na-adj)<br/>
<br/>
Note: It is not usually placed after verbs as they are usually in the 'masu' form unless there is a change to the verb. E.g. the '-tai' (want to...) form; the verb is put into polite negative past form - '-masendeshita'. <br/>
<br/>
When translated to english, it can mean, 'is' or 'am' etc. <br/>
<br/>
To change to past tense, 'desu' becomes 'deshita'. <br/>
<br/>
Note: Adjectives do not have 'deshita' to imply past tense. You would change the adjectives themselves into past tense and then add 'desu'.<br/>
<br/>
<b>Examples</b><br/>
I am a Japanese person. (noun)<br/>
日本人です。<br/>
<br/>
It is a melon. (noun)<br/>
メロンです。<br/>
<br/>
It was a melon. (past, noun)<br/>
メロンでした。<br/>
<br/>
I'm Talon. (noun)<br/>
私はタロンです。<br/>
<br/>
It's cute. (I-adj)<br/>
かわいいです。<br/>
<br/>
It's famous. (Na-adj)<br/>
有名です。
