## Meaning

as expected,  after all

he studied hard and passed, as expected

## Notes

### 1 — dc

see <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> for examples, explanation. identical.

### 2 — rubyhatchet

だけに can be used for both good and bad merits. However, だけあって is used for good merits. It should not be used for negative nuances.

## Discussion

### 1 — Miki

ex<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=dakeni#">#</a></b> can be read as "only in Tokyo world-wide cuisine is available."

### 2 — bamboo4

To avoid that, I have added やっぱり.<br/>

### 3 — nhk9

I have seen Dakeni in several Level 2 grammar books before, so I think it would be safe to say that Dakeni should be a Level 2 expression, instead of level 1.

### 4 — dc

nhk9 - updated thanks.

### 5 — benni

[ex<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=dakeni#">#</a></b>] This may be picky, but I disagree with the translation. If I am mistaken please correct me as it is important for other learners to understand. "Her parents are living alone in the countryside. (As one would expect) Since they are getting older, as a daughter she is worried (about them)."

### 6 — yadokari

I asked a native speaker and she said benni was right. for ex 6176 the translation should read<br/>
"Her parents are living together in the countriside. Since they are getting older, she is worrying about them."<br/>
the daughter is the one doing the worrying.

### 7 — karekano

I've changed ex <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=dakeni#">#</a></b> from: "(Her) parents are living together in the countryside. As expected of those getting older, they worry about their daughter"; to: "(Her) parents are living together in the countryside. Since they are getting older, she is worrying about them." I've marked it as suspicious. Please, some native speaker correct it ^^ thank you.
