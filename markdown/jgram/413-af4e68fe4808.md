## Meaning

just as I'd,  right at the moment when I'd

just as I thought to call her, she called me

## Notes

### 1 — rubyhatchet

「Aたところ、Bた」: B is the subsequent result or acquired knowledge, et cetera, after doing A. This is a one-time-only action &amp; result.<br/>
<br/>
Vた + ところ<br/>
* the most common use is 〜てみたところ

### 2 — sureshnihon

〜たら　＜あることをしたら，その結果がどうなったかを浮ｷ阜ｻ。＞

## Discussion

### 1 — dc

often has a result implied ~tara

### 2 — Amatuka

＾たところ implies you'd just done (e.g. past tense) something.  See also ^るところ for just as I was about to.

### 3 — Amatuka

Don't forget that V-たところ can be a literal place where V-た took place. e.g. パーティーをやったところはどこだっけ。'Where was it you had the party again?'

### 4 — dc

there seems to be a big overlap between "just as", and "when" here. I think tatokoro is much more the former, with the emphasis.

### 5 — Amatuka

Still don't know what you mean by 'result implied'.

### 6 — Amatuka

If you mean that there should be <b>different tokoro entries</b> for 'just when' vs 'when' then I disagree.  I don't think <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>-たところ has as strong an implication of 'instant' as (for instance) とたん.

### 7 — Amatuka

### 8 — Miki

It is a bit natural to think the 「ところ」as a place due to the part 店から出てきた。<br/>

### 11 — bamboo4

I would say that ところで more or less has location-orientation whereas ところを would have temporal-orientation. Still contrary construction is possible for both of these phrases. I would wager that most of the authors most probably would not have used either of these phrases with any degree of conscious effort to have it meant either way.

### 12 — 誠

1.volitional form + としたところ＝to be on the point of...／going to/about to/on the verge of (doing something) but just before doing<br/>
<br/>
   Has a similar meaning to 〜(す)るところ＝今からすぐする（まだしていない）<br/>
E.g. : 引っ越そうとしたところです。<br/>
　　　　I am on the point of moving.<br/>
       これから、説明するところです。<br/>
　　　　From now on, I am going to explain.<br/>
<br/>
２．〜(し)ているところ＝just started doing 〜<br/>
今ちょうどしている（まだ終わっていない）<br/>
E.g. 今、お風呂に入っているところだから、後で電話　　　してください。<br/>
　　　Now I am just having my bath, so please <br/>
      phone me later.<br/>
<br/>
3. 〜たところ＝just finished 〜ing<br/>
  ＝今終わったばかり <br/>
E.g. : 病気が治ったところなので、まだ泳ぐことができない。<br/>
　　　Since he had just recovered from the illness, therefore he still can't swim.<br/>
<br/>
４．Another very similar meaning of 〜たところ＝<br/>
　　〜たらその結果＝once (he/she) had 〜<br/>
E.g. : 電話で問い合わせたところ、休館日は月曜日だということです。<br/>
　　Once I had enquired by phone, I was told that the off day is Monday.<br/>
<br/>
5. 〜ている／ていたところに＝just at that moment, one is/was/were about to(present / past tense depends on the sentence)<br/>
 　also under 〜ところに／へ／を＝〜という状況、場面に／へ／を<br/>
<br/>
E.g. : 出掛けようとしているところに、電話がかかってきた。<br/>
      Just when I was about to go out, the phone came.<br/>
       一人で寂しくテレビを見ているところへ、友達が訪ねてきた。<br/>
　　　Just when I was watching the TV, my friend came to visit me.<br/>
        恋人と話しているところを、先生にみられちゃった。<br/>
　　　　Just when I was talking to my lover, my teacher caught an eye of us/we were spotted by the teacher.<br/>
<br/>
６．Another meaning of 〜ところ(を)＝〜という状態なのに、（挨拶で相手に対してよく使われる）<br/>
   = although, despite (polite sense)<br/>
<br/>
E.g.: お忙しいところをわざわざおいで頂き、誠にありがとうございました。<br/>
　　 I would like to thank you sincerely for coming despite being busy.
