## Discussion

### 1 — deweychetham

真逆 [まさか] (adv) (uk) by no means, (P)<br/>
まさか (adv) never!, well, I never!, you don't say!<br/>
Literally:<br/>
真 [ま] (n,pref) just, right, due (east), pure, genuine, true, (P)<br/>
逆 [ぎゃく] (adj-na,n) reverse, opposite, (P)<br/>
逆さ [さかさ] (n) reverse, inversion, upside down, (P)<br/>
In English: "directly opposite"<br/>
<br/>
<br/>

### 2 — bamboo4

まさか coupled with negative or denial expresses unexpected hypothetical case. Synonimous with よもや or いくらなんでも.<br/>

### 3 — yookoso

V-plain + とは思う is used in most of the examples above - does that deserve its own JGram entry?

### 4 — nhk9

I think using only まさか can have the connotation of "no way"... the "nai" is often left unsaid.  <br/>
<br/>
ex:<br/>
A: 彼がもう結婚しちゃったって<br/>
B: まさか(そういうことあるわけないだろう)

### 5 — 誠

### 6 — xabre24

I agree with yookosoさん, how does V-plain +とは思う differ from V-plain +と思う/だと思う？

### 7 — KWhazit

Another phrase I like to use when translating まさか is "Don't tell me ... !", though that doesn't work in every case.<br/>
<br/>
And a まさか phrase that doesn't translate literally is そのまさかだ！ (or the equivalent). Sometimes, when someone says, for example, まさか、(insert unlikely/unwelcome situation here)じゃないだろう？ (It COULDN'T be (situation), right?), the response is そのまさかだ！ (It IS that (situation)!). Try translating まさか itself directly in that case, and you're just asking for trouble.

I like the translation "hardly" for masaka.
