## Meaning

...said; no matter what

He said that.....

## Notes

### 1 — jiji\_the\_cat

って　can also take the place (and often does) of は when designating a subject.  Used in casual speech.

## Discussion

### 1 — darg

this looks to me like 2 different usages- って as a contraction of と or という, and って as a contraction of でも, both of which are more informal then their counterparts.

### 2 — bamboo4

I never heard of って as a contraction of でも.

### 3 — darg

For example, なんでも can become なんだって and やっても can become やったって in everyday speech without changing the meaning. The best way I could think of to say it was to call it でも.

### 6 — KyleGoetz

@ami I'll have to use Romaji because my computer won't switch to Japanese for some reason right now.<br/>
~ tte no wa<br/>
is functionally equivalent to<br/>
~ to iu no wa<br/>
in that it means "~ is defined as", basically. For example:<br/>
"Taberu" tte no wa tabemono wo kuti ni ireru koto de aru.<br/>
"Eating" means "putting food into your mouth."<br/>
I personally find it more pleasing to say "~tte wa" without the "no", or just "~tte".

### 9 — Ashish Joshi

@nnskage <br/>
yes friend there is a rule for the usage of って and て .<br/>
てis used when the following verb is used with ん  sound.<br/>
For ex-あれはなんてよむんですか.<br/>
And, って   is used when the following verb does not consist of the ん  sound.<br/>
For ex-あそこ で みどりぐちって かいて あります ね.
