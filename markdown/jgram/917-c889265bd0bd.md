## Meaning

while doing A, I had a chance to do B, since doing A anyway, do B

while i was at my parents,

## Notes

### 1 — dc

see <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> for explanations and similar grammar

### 2 — rubyhatchet

「Aついでに、B」: A is the main action and B is another action done while peforming A.<br/>
<br/>
V(dict. form)・(plain past form) + ついでに<br/>
N + の + ついでに

## Discussion

### 1 — dc

I might have thought this was usually used for past things, but it can also be used for the future.<br/>
　パリへ行くついでに、ロンドンに寄る<br/>
　Since I am going to go to Paris, I will also stop by London

### 2 — dc

V-plain or V-plainpast + ついでに

### 3 — dc

implies at the same time

### 7 — LittleFish

After doing some Googling, I think it means something close to "While you're at it", but much more flexable (ie, not limited to present or "you").<br/>
I saw it at the beginning of a sentance. The person requested that someone teach him how a broadcasting system of some sort worked, and the sentance ended. Then the next began with "序でに," and it was requested that a related concept also be explained.

### 8 — dc

this page <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> tries to explain some subtleties of nuance...

### 9 — Leslie

can you use this as a standalone casual expression to mean "while you're at it...?"

### 10 — blabby

I have heard as a standalone expression "あとついでに、〜する". "Oh, while I'm here I'll do 〜". <br/>
If example, if you are in a convenience store and you suddenly remember to buy mints. "Ah, while I'm here, I'll buy some mints"

### 11 — nitrate

Is the nuance a bit like the English expression: "killing 2 birds with 1 stone"?

### 12 — KWhazit

The connection to "two birds with one stone" seems a bit on the weak side to me, but not entirely absent. I think the two birds saying has more to do with deliberately combining two tasks, while ついでに makes the second one more of an afterthought.<br/>
<br/>
I'd explain AついでにB as, A is the main thing I'm doing, and B wasn't necessarily even in my plans, but doing A makes it convenient to do B, so I might as well do B too.
