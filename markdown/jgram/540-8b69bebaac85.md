## Meaning

Can't help but 〜; Extremely 〜; Very 〜

Due to a lack of sleep, I can't help but be exhausted.

## Notes

### 1 — rubyhatchet

＊しようがない is also acceptable sometimes, but this grammar is much more widely written as しょうがない.<br/>
<br/>
＊てたまらない is stronger than てしょうがない, but the two are otherwise interchangeable.<br/>
<br/>
＊てしょうがない should express feelings, sentiments, mentality, or desire. For example:<br/>
1. 疲れる -&gt; 疲れてしょうがない<br/>
2. 痛い -&gt; 痛くてしようがない・痛くてたまらない<br/>
3. 残念 -&gt; 残念でしようがない・残念でたまらない<br/>
<br/>
FORMATION:<br/>
Ｖ・いＡ（て forms) + しょうがない・たまらない<br/>
なＡ + で + しょうがない・たまらない

## Discussion

### 1 — your name

shouldn't it be "shiyo ga nai", not shou ga nai,

### 2 — Miki

According to Daijirin, both ways are ok.

### 3 — dc

### 4 — Miki

しよう（仕様）がない（無い）

### 5 — bamboo4

The title should be "shiyou ga nai" and て should be deleted.<br/>
I have added an example. NOte that しようがない is commonly pronounced as "shoo ga nai."<br/>

### 6 — bamboo4

<b><a href="http://jgram.org:80/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=teshouganai;+tetamaranai#5458">#5458</a></b> in Japanese is weird. Also, in <b><a href="http://jgram.org:80/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=teshouganai;+tetamaranai#276">#276</a></b>, "tired" should be "sleepy."

### 7 — rubyhatchet

しょうがない on its own is a sort of idiom meaning "Can't be helped," but because this is a grammar entry, it should be emphasized that しょうがない is correctly used in a sentence when it follows て or で. So, てしょうがない is a proper grammar entry title. In that case, example <b><a href="http://jgram.org:80/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=teshouganai;+tetamaranai#939">#939</a></b> is simply a conversational use of the idiom and doesn't belong in this entry.
