### 10 — Miki

I think と言ったら is almost the same as と言うと but makes a sentence more casual and colloquial. <br/>
ex<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=toiuto#">#</a></b> というと is different from others. 何か というと should be used a set. 何か_と_いったら means different.<br/>
何かといったら、犬に決まっているじゃないか。

### 11 — nhk9

I don't really know what the meanings for both 何か というとnor the meaning for 何か_と_いったら.  However, I think there's a slight difference between toiuto and toittara.  Both means about "speaking of..." but the former is just stating something in a neutral sense, whereas the latter one is stating something more of surprise, or beauty.<br/>
<br/>
So I think it's more common to say this "沖縄といったら、文字でかけないほど美しい浜辺や海がたくさんあるようだね"　with toittara than with toiuto.  I found this in the Kanzen Masuta textbook for level 2 grammar.

### 12 — bamboo4

といったら is used when making a conclusive or definitive statement, allowing nothing else, whereas というと is more generic and allows many alternatives.<br/>
Compare 日本の山といったら富士山だ(when you say which is THE mountain in Japan, it would have to be Mt. Fuji) with the example <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=toiuto#">#</a></b> above.

### 13 — LittleFish

I've heard "何故と言ったら" before in a few lectures. The speaker used "なぜと言ったら..." to push the lecture in a further direction, (seeing as there was no audience participation to tell him to keep going), to keep it flowing. Almost as if to say " "Why?" you may ask; well, blah blah blah."

### 14 — KyleGoetz

nhk9 is absolutely correct. かといったら contains within it a feeling, emotion, or surprise, while かというと does not. Straight from the ALC literature for ２級.

### 20 — tigert

I was just wondering if this と言うと　isn`t really a single grammar point. と言う is often used to give an explanation of something. It basicly means "to say"<br/>
<br/>
IE　何と言う学校で勉強しますか？<br/>
　What is the school you study at called?<br/>
<br/>
With that as it is. the final と before the rest of the sentence is the sentence connective causative と that implies "because of S1, Naturally S2 came to be" <br/>
<br/>
so Ex 378 is really<br/>
When (Someone `omitted は`) says "Switzerland", what image do you see (Naturally without the ablity to control it `と`)<br/>
<br/>
<br/>
Ex 3109 can be broken down to a rhetorical Question followed by the (natural and unchangable) answer.<br/>
<br/>
Ex 3115 isn`t a very good translation. "sorry bamboo"<br/>
<br/>
何か　means something so 何かと言う means to say something so a better translation would be<br/>
<br/>
when ever (anyone　`omitted は`) says something, ( it is naturally unavoidable that `と`) that guy will open ( let it appear `出る`) his mouth. <br/>
