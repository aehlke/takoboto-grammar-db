## Meaning

In order to do ぜひ実現させたい積極的な目的をもってあることをする

In order to pass the test, he studied day and night.

## Notes

### 1 — claytonian

Formal.<br/>
Written more than spoken.

### 2 — claytonian

にせんがため is equivalent to にするため

### 3 — claytonian

uses the nai form of verbs without nai.<br/>
In the case of suru, it is, as in the name of the entry, せんがため.

### 4 — blutorange

Actually, this has nothing to do with ない etymologically. This construction consists of a verb in 未然形, to which the classical verb む (uncertainty, future) is added, and shortened to ん. が means almost the same as の modern Japanese. ため is obviously 為.<br/>
As for 未然形+む, another shortening was mu -&gt; u. Together with other sound shifts, this actually constitutes the etymology of the well known 食べよう・飲もう form. It goes 「taberu &gt; tabe+mu &gt; tabeu &gt; tabeyou」 and 「nomu &gt; noma+mu &gt; nomau &gt; nomou」.
