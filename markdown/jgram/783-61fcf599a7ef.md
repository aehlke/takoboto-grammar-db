## Discussion

### 1 — Amatuka

Useful Japanese page on this is <a href="http://homepage3.nifty.com/i-yasu/Lesson11.htm">here</a>

### 2 — Amatuka

Formed from -te form verb + iru (e.g. 行く → 行っている)<br/>
<br/>
Vte = -te form verb.

### 3 — Amatuka

Dropping the い <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> is a common abbreviation.<br/>
(e.g.  行っている -&gt;  行ってる [itteiru → itteru])

### 4 — Amatuka

There are quite a few differrent usages of this form,<br/>
1. Ongoing action<br/>
公園で子どもが遊んでいる。 [kouen de kodomo ga asondeiru.]<br/>
(The) children are playing at the park.<br/>
2. Ongoing state (result of an action)<br/>
エアコンがついている。[eakon ga tsuiteiru.]<br/>
The airconditioner is on.<br/>
3. (as negative) Action not <b>yet</b> taken.<br/>
まだ昼ご飯を食べていない。[mada hirugohan wo tabeteinai.]<br/>
I haven't had lunch yet.<br/>
(Note this is different from 昼ご飯を食べない。[hirugohan wo tabenai.] I'm not going to eat lunch.<br/>
4. Experience<br/>
本を書いてる。[hon wo kaiteru.] I've written a book.<br/>
5. Repetative actions.<br/>
僕はよく映画館に行っている。[boku ha yoku eigakan ni itteiru.]<br/>
I often go to the cinema.<br/>
6. Condition<br/>
彼は太っている。[kare ha futotteiru.]<br/>
He's fat.<br/>

### 5 — Amatuka

ている <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> can't be used with the verbs of existance (e.g. ある <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> and いる <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>)

### 6 — tataku

2. Ongoing state (result of an action)<br/>
  エアコンがついている。[eakon ga tsuiteiru.]<br/>
  The airconditioner is on.<br/>
Note that this use is only with intransitive verbs. If it were the transitive form of this verb (tsukeru, rather than tsuku), it would be -tearu (eakon ga tsuketearu).

### 10 — tigert

Hey ミサ, I asked my coworker what the diffrence is and he confirmed your guess. <br/>
<br/>
待って　is something that is currently being done and 待ってて is something that will be started after the completion of something else. <br/>
<br/>
IE if your teacher askes for your homework and you have to dig in your backpack to get it, 待ってください<br/>
<br/>
if you are in the middle of completing your homework. 待っててください。<br/>
<br/>
I give you 10 points for posting this question. I have always used both interchangable (not a problem if you do) but its always good to learn a little more about the smaller meanings within the words.
