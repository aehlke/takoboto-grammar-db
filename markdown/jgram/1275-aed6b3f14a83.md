## Meaning

instead of milk, sweetener

### 3 — jmadsen

Question: My grammar book says に かわり instead of かわりに for all its examples and explanations. ex. 首相　に　かわり、　外相　が　アメリカ　を　訪問　した。<br/>
Can anyone clarify?

### 4 — jmadsen

Just got my question answered :-)<br/>
ni kawari is more "polite" or "official", and also shows a more passive sense of the "ni" particle. kawari + ni is more typical in spoken language. So, these two sentences are the same in meaning:<br/>
相　に　かわり、　外相　が　アメリカ　を　訪問　した。<br/>
相　の　かわり　に、　外相　が　アメリカ　を　訪問　した。<br/>
But the first is more likely to occur in a news report.

### 5 — bluntcrayon

can you even use い adjectives with this form of かわりに?<br/>
I know it can be used for the other form , i.e some form of compensation or balance　(E.g.　高い代わりに便利だった）, but all the books i have read including the dictionary of int grammar, state it cant be used in a way such as 高い代わりに  （instead of being expensive)
