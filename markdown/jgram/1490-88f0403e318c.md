## Meaning

like / ish ish

his sarcastic-ish way of speaking

## Notes

### 1 — dc

means "-ish" or "steeped in". hard to translate!

### 2 — loull

これはうまくいくだろうと確信めいたものを感じていた。<br/>
I felt sure that this would work out.

## Discussion

### 1 — dc

what does this mean? example:　<br/>
山は春めく<br/>

### 2 — blabby

This grammar is quite difficult because it is hard to translate into English.  The best translation I can come up with is<br/>
<br/>
* to become ~like  (山は春めく - "The mountain became spring-like")<br/>
* to show signs of (show signs of spring)<br/>
* ~ish (他人めく - "strangerish")？<br/>
* ~ly (冗談めく - Jokingly/皮肉めく - sarcastically)<br/>
* ~ing (謎めく - Puzzling)

### 3 — blabby

Another interesting thing about this grammar is its use with 擬音語/擬態語<br/>
<br/>
きらきらする  = きらめく To glitter<br/>
ひらひらする  = ひらめく To flash/flutter<br/>
どきどきする? = ときめく To pulpitate<br/>
<br/>
If anyone can correct this or has more information, I would love to hear it.

### 4 — dc

きらめく、ときめく　are totally different animals from 皮肉めいた、他人めいた。

### 5 — Miki

名詞や副詞、形容詞や形容動詞の語幹base＋めく means like something or being in a condtion of something.

### 8 — Miki

That's just the transformation of natsumeku 夏めく. Yes, all means "summer-like" すっかり夏めいてまいりました。皆様いかがお過ごしでしょうか。 Natsumeku here is used as compliments of the season for June in a letter.
