## Meaning

"thought so, but" OR, "would have thought so/imagined, but didn't" 想像したろうか、いや、しなかった。思ったろうか、いや、思わなかった。

I thought this restaurant would be cheap but (surprisingly) the bill was over 5,000 yen.

## Notes

### 1 — Sasato

This is a grammar I learned out of a book and was surprised to actually hear it used in real life on a number of occasions. You use this grammar when you think one thing, but much to your astonishment, things actually turn out to be very different. You use it in the same way as you would express any thoughts, by using the quotation 「と」 and 「思う」. The only difference is that you use 「思いきや」 instead of 「思う」. There is no tense in 「思いきや」, or rather, since the results have already went against your expectations, the original thought is implicitely understood to be past tense. (from Taekim)

### 2 — LR

<b>Ref <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=toomoikiya#">#</a></b></b> Kanzen Master Level 1 - p25 - no.28

### 3 — Tosiaki

き is a particle indicating past tense, and や is a particle meaning "but" "however" etc. This is a leftover from older Japanese, since both are no longer commonly used in modern Japanese except in this particular phrase.

### 7128 — Sasato

				昼間だから絶対込んでいる<strong>と思いきや</strong>、一人もいなかった。<strong></strong></a> <br/>
<strong>Despite having thought </strong>
		that it must be crowded since it was afternoon, (surprisingly)  not a single person was there

### 7896 — LR

<a href="http://www.csse.monash.edu.au/~jwb/cgi-bin/wwwjdic.cgi?9MHJ雪国で育った彼のことだから、さぞかしスキーがうまいだろうと思いきや、滑っては転び、滑っては転びの繰り返しだった">

		<strong>you'd think </strong>
		he'd be great at skiing, but he just slips and falls, slips and falls.

		In my remaining years 
		<strong>I thought I could </strong>

### 7898 — LR

<a href="http://www.csse.monash.edu.au/~jwb/cgi-bin/wwwjdic.cgi?9MHJA チームの勝利で試合終了かと思いきや、最後に大逆転が起こった。">

		the tournament would end with a win for team A, but surprisingly, it ended in a huge turn in the opposite direction.
