## Meaning

particle denoting uncertainty or a listing used for both words and  clauses

such as...and (?)

## Discussion

### 1 — Nabeshin

### 2 — Aes

### 3 — lou1sb

I disagree, やら　やら　seems to translate into English as 'Things like...' or 'Do things like...' while や、や、など translates more accurately as 'and, and, etc.'<br/>
<br/>
など　states explicitely that there are further things in the category, やら　やら　only implies it.

### 5 — Socky

Apparently, 'やら' is similar to using 'や' or 'とか' but indicates less certainty, and also has other uses as well. For example:<br/>
"彼のクローゼットには、帽子やスカーフはありました。"<br/>
"In his closet, there were things like caps and scarves."<br/>
as opposed to:<br/>
"彼のクローゼットには、帽子やらスカーフはありました。"<br/>
"In his closet, (I'm fairly certain... I think) there were things like caps and scarves (or something like that)."<br/>
<br/>
Although it apparently (according to The Handbook of Japanese Grammar by Harold G. Henderson) has other usages, in this case this is how it would be used. I heard it used in the sentence:<br/>
「試してみるのか？お前が言う光とやらの力を？」<br/>
I take this to mean:<br/>
"Are you trying this? The power of this(uncertain of meaning)light you speak of?"<br/>
Apparently とやら is the same as やら. I'm not really sure, there doesn't seem to be much info on this anywhere.<br/>
***EDIT: とやら actually means "and the like" and works similarly to "nado" but once again, with less certainty and more of that "something-like-that" nuance. So the previous sentence actually should translate to:<br/>
"The power of this 'light' (and such/and the like/ and stuff like that) that you speak of."<br/>
<br/>
To be honest, it sounds like "and stuff like that" would be the closest definition. The words you are listing are said with partial certainty, and like the previous commenter said, や  and とか can often be translated to "and."
