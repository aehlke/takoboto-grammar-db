## Meaning

1) just, only, simply, merely; 2) as much as X wants/like/can (whatever, anything)

1) don't just love her; 2) eat as much as you want (eat whatever/anything you'd like)

## Notes

### 1 — ziaz

There are several different uses of dake and different meanings as well. <br/>
1) only;just;simply;merely<br/>
2) as ... as possible; whatever; anything<br/>
出来るだけ早く＝as quickly as possible<br/>

### 2 — rubyhatchet

As for the second use of だけ (〜 as much/long/many as you/I/etc (verb)), note that you only use verbs for this grammar, just as you do in English:<br/>
<br/>
Ｖplain form + だけ<br/>
Ｖpotential form + だけ<br/>
<br/>
As for other miscellaneous forms that do not use verbs, the most commonly used ones include:<br/>
〜(し)たいだけ<br/>
ほしいだけ<br/>
好きなだけ

### 3 — Marcel

ほしいだけ<br/>
as much as you want<br/>
好きなだけ<br/>
as much as you like<br/>
できるだけ<br/>
as much as you can

### 1 — Amatuka

Lots of common usages such as "not only XXX but YYY" "XXXだけでなく、YYYだ。"

### 2 — Amatuka

First example : Lit. "She is not only diligent she is also charming"

### 3 — Amatuka

### 4 — Jimmie

I think that's almost correct, DC.<br/>
<br/>
But I rarely hear ３人しかないので and ３人しかないでも.<br/>
I'd rather say ３人しかいなかったので、大変だった and<br/>
３人だけしかいないけれど十分 or ３人だけでも十分.<br/>
<br/>
It's significantly difficult to explain because<br/>
this kind of sentences totally depends on personal<br/>
feelings...<br/>
<br/>
しか　〜　ない implies negative feelings, and だけ<br/>
implies positive feelings, that's that.<br/>
<br/>
