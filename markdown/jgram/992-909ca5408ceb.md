## Meaning

it seems,  apparently,  somehow

it seems he doesn't know

## Discussion

### 1 — Miki

As for the first example, J should be どうやら彼_は_分からないらしい。<br/>
彼が分からないらしい would be, it seems she doesn't know who he is.

### 2 — bamboo4

どうやら彼が分からないらしい means someone (who remains unidentified but not the speaker) cannot discern him.<br/>
<br/>
どうやら彼は分からないらしい means "he" is not able to discern something (not dicclosed) as observed by the speaker.   <br/>
<br/>

### 3 — bamboo4

Accordingly, 彼が分からないらしい should be changed to 彼は知らないらしい.<br/>
<br/>
<br/>

### 4 — yookoso

Is the 'somehow' meaning similar to nantoka (なんとか)? If so, there is no entry for that currently...

ex<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=douyara#">#</a></b>, 3933 and 3934 どうやら can be replaced by なんとか.<br/>

### 9 — Nick

〜らしい (I hear that... / Someone says that...)<br/>
〜ようだ (It seems that...)<br/>
These are not interchangeable.

### 10 — Petra

Perhaps I don't understand. This is what I think from these two phrases:<br/>
<br/>
どうやら、私はバスの中で財布を盗まれたようだ。<br/>
It looks like I had my wallet stolen on the bus somehow. (The person has noticed their wallet is missing all on their own.)<br/>
<br/>
どうやら、私はバスの中で財布を盗まれたらしい<br/>
I heard/It seems that I had my wallet stolen on the bus somehow. <br/>
(They don't remember what happened on the bus, but someone has told them later they saw the wallet being stolen. Or maybe someone has told them their wallet was stolen before they noticed it themselves. However, I don't think I would have the nerve to tell someone I saw their wallet being stolen yet didn't say it at the time it was happening...)<br/>
<br/>
Please correct me if I'm wrong.

### 11 — jmadsen

Please note that どうやら<br/>
is used with -らしい, -みたい, -そうだ, etc.<br/>
<br/>
To use どうやら by itself is, in the words of my wife, "strange"<br/>
<br/>
