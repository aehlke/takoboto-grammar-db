## Meaning

No sooner than; just as,  at that instant

just as the doors opened they rushed in

## Notes

### 1 — 赤毛

This grammar also gives the sense that X was not sure if action A had or hadn't occurred/started/finished before the subsequent action B commenced.<br/>
(note copied from Groove note in sister entry "ka nai ka no uchi ni")

## Discussion

### 1 — Amatuka

This usage seems to be fairly rare - most of the references to it are from Japanese grammar sites &amp; similar.

### 2 — dc

its usually used for a small instant of time.

### 3 — dc

LIT: in the time between the doors not being open and them opening...

### 4 — Miki

You would see this expression in written word. I don't often use this in a converation as I would bite my tongue. Instead I say, 電車のドアが開いた瞬間に、--。

### 5 — dc

瞬間=しゅんかん ?<br/>

### 6 — Amatuka

瞬間＝しゅんかん

### 7 — Amatuka

I think this one should be good to go.  Any problems or should we revise the check mark up?

### 8 — bamboo4

I agree that 電車のドアが 開く か 開かないかのうちに is a mouthful and an ordinary Japanese would say 電車のドアが開いたかどうかのうちに. Recommend that this item is modified accordingly.<br/>
