## Meaning

use Nounが instead of Nounに これがそれに代わって

instead of this, use that

## Notes

### 1 — Viola

meishi + ni kawatte

### 2 — tigert

the base of this grammar point is the polite request form [V-てform　ください] <br/>
<br/>
the dictionary form of かわって is かわる which means to substatute or to change from one thing to another. <br/>
<br/>
example:<br/>
<br/>
今日は　大嫌いケーキが私の一番好きなのにかわりました。<br/>
<br/>
Today, My favorit cake was substatued with one I hate.<br/>
<br/>
The basic form of this is <br/>
Noun+ga Noun+ni kawaru<br/>
<br/>
Noun+に was replaced by Noun+が<br/>
<br/>
In order to change this to a request we simply change kawaru to a request form<br/>
<br/>
Example:<br/>
代わって　下さい　Please　substatute it<br/>

## Discussion

### 1 — anon

based on the examples, it seems that the description "instead of" isn't complete, maybe you should ad "for" or would it be more accurate to say "for the benefit of <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>"?

### 2 — anon

Actually i consulted a native speaker, and apparently <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> is mainly used for an "on behalf of" type phrase, whereas <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> is mostly for "instead of". <br/>
However, the examples we have here only seem to give the "on behalf of" sense...

### 3 — scorduan

It sounds like "in the place of" captures the nuances best, as translated in sentence 2. It's substitutionary. <br/>
<br/>
Here, in the place of humans, robots are doing the work.<br/>
<br/>
Jim  wrote the letter, in Betty's place.<br/>
<br/>
I'll do everything in your place, so you only have to wait here.

### 4 — Fritz

In the examples above, the に noun is the one that gets replaced. But the "Meaning" and "Notes" sections (at the time when I am writing) say that the に noun does the replacing (that the が noun gets replaced). From what I have seen elsewhere, the examples are correct, and the explanations have it backwards.
