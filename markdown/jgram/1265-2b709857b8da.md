## Meaning

while doing something

getting one thing done while doing something else

## Notes

### 1 — colinp123

<b>[がてら]</b><br/>
Used to express getting one thing accomplished while doing a second thing. Perhaps a good English equivalent would be "to kill two birds with one stone."

### 2 — Miki

gatera can be reaplced with <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>ついでに or wo<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> を兼ねて.<br/>
○散歩ついでに、本屋に寄った。<br/>
○散歩を兼ねて、本屋に寄った。<br/>
ｘ散歩をしながら、本屋に寄った。This is not natural.<br/>
I think that がてら is used to show another action （本屋に寄る） taken place in the middle of one main action （散歩をする）,  散歩→本屋→散歩<br/>
While ながら、two or more actions are at the same time. <br/>
eg. 友だちと喋りながら歩く。 I walk as talking with a friend.　歩く=喋る

### 10 — Iso

がてら has two meanings. <br/>
1.verb + がてら + A<br/>
  In this case, がてら is used to show another action(A) taken place in the middle of one main action. This can be replaced with ついでに, but not with 兼ねて.<br/>
<br/>
2. noun(A) + がてら + B<br/>
 In this case, it can be replaced with ついでに or 兼ねて. But be carefull, there's a subtle difference between the two. <br/>
ついでに = doing B while doing A<br/>
兼ねて = doing B with the intention of doing A

### 11 — mc

In the book くらべてわかる中級日本語阜ｻ文型ドリル, they talk about the fact that it depends on the location where the 2 actions take place.<br/>
The 2 actions take place in same or different location -&gt; AついでにB<br/>
The 2 actions take place in different location -&gt; AがてらB(unless the main action implies a movement or displacement 〜しに行く)<br/>
Also the 2nd meaning of AがてらB could be "doing B will help to do A" and also only ついでに can be used at the beginning of a sentence.<br/>
But the two expressions can mean "killing 2 birds with one stone".<br/>
<br/>
In fact, after reading many exemple sentences, I think that the difference between がてら and ついでに<br/>
doesn't lie in the meaning (beside the 2nd meaning of がてら).<br/>
Most of the time they mean the same. What makes me choose one over the other is how I construct the sentence, which words I use and how the whole sentence sounds.<br/>
In the end, I believe one shouldn't try too hard to build a rule to differentiate them.<br/>
If a rule is not obvious enough or contains too much exceptions, then it shouldn't be used as a rule in the first place.<br/>
I think it's one of those cases where you have 2 ways for saying the same thing, which is great so when you talk, you don't sound all the same.
