## Meaning

passive verb ending

the water was drunk by the child

## Notes

### 1 — dc

Formation<br/>
<br/>
Verb (nai stem) ＋　れる・られる<br/>
<br/>
(Grp I)<br/>
　〜れる <br/>
　話す　→　話さ.れる (be told)<br/>
　聞く　→　聞かれる  (be heard)<br/>
<br/>
(Grp II)<br/>
　〜られる <br/>
　食べる　→　食べ.られる (be eaten)<br/>
　立てる　→　立て.られる (be built)<br/>
<br/>
(irreg)<br/>
　来る　→　来られる<br/>
　する　→　される

### 2 — dc

Compare the passive and active examples below<br/>
<br/>
(active) <br/>
　子供が水を飲みました。<br/>
　The child drank the water<br/>
(passive) <br/>
　水が子供に飲まれました。<br/>
　the water was drunk by the child

### 3 — dc

The passive of group 2 verbs is the same as the potential and honorific. Which can only be discovered by the context:<br/>
<br/>
先生は納豆&lt;b&gt;が&lt;/b&gt;食べられる<br/>
　the が gives this away as potential<br/>
potential: My teacher can eat nattou<br/>
<br/>
先生は納豆を食べられた<br/>
　could be many things:<br/>
<br/>
honorific: my Teacher ate nattou<br/>
potential: my teacher could eat nattou<br/>
indirect passive: someone ate natto (~and my teacher was unhappy about it)

### 4 — dc

There is also an indirect passive, which doesn't exist in English. It implies the other person is annoyed by the action.<br/>
<br/>
Perhaps it could be translated as "person A did something AT person B"<br/>
<br/>
ビールは田中に飲まれた<br/>
the beer was drunk by Tanaka<br/>
<br/>
先生は田中にビールを飲まれた<br/>
(The teacher was drunk beer at by Tanaka)<br/>
The teacher was annoyed by Taro drinking beer.

### 5 — dc

some more formation examples<br/>
<br/>
For 一段　（いちだん　−　える、いる　ending) verbs you essentially drop the る and tack on a　られる. <br/>
<br/>
For 五段　（ごだん　〜う　ending) verbs you change the last syllable so that it ends <br/>
in 〜あ　and add　れる　 <br/>
<br/>
話す　　話される  was spoken<br/>
聞く　　聞かれる  was listened/heard<br/>
泳ぐ　　泳がれる  was swum<br/>
待つ　　待たれる  was carried<br/>
死ぬ　　死なれる  was killed<br/>
会う　　会われる　was met<br/>
作る　　作られる  was made<br/>
呼ぶ　　呼ばれる  was called

### 6 — bamboo4

In Japanese, you can use passive voice either for transitive or intransitive verbs. When you use passive voice for intransitive verbs, it normally indicate some kind of disadvantage for the speaker.For example, 居る can be changed to 居られる but this would normally indicate you don't want him to be there, but he is there nevertheless.<br/>
<br/>
Thus it is possible to say 犬に水を飲まれました, which gave you the disadvantage of the dog having drank <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> water, but I said it is awkward because one would normally have one's dog under control so that that kind of thing would not happen. The result is that you are declaring your own stupidity by making that statement. <br/>

### 9 — starsandsea

According to my textbook, you can also use transitive verbs to indicate a disadvantage. (The book calls this kind of passive sentence "indirect passive sentences.")<br/>
<br/>
Transitive verb example:<br/>
となりの学生にテストを見られました。<br/>
(My test was looked at by the student next to me and it troubled me.)<br/>
<br/>
Intransitive verb example:<br/>
（私は）雨に降られました。<br/>
(It rained and it troubled me.)
