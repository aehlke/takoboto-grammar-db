## Meaning

to begin/start VERB-ing ある活動を始める

I started reading yesterday.

## Notes

### 1 — KyleGoetz

This construct expresses the beginning of an action or verb. It takes the [conjunctive form] of the verb and appends to it the auxiliary verb 始める.

### 2 — Sakuratree

Hajimaru which also means to start same as hajimeru means the same.<br/>
<br/>
However, hajimaru doesn't require someone to do something and it never takes the を particle while hajimeru needs を.<br/>
<br/>
Japanese contains these types of verbs: transitive and intransitive.

### 5 — arkofnoah

出す have a nuance of sudden action<br/>
<br/>
走り出す means to "break into a run", like when a girl sees a cockroach and she "走り出す"。<br/>
<br/>
始めた have a more neutral meaning, as in started eating, or start running in the case of "走り始めた" (like you start running when a race start.)<br/>
<br/>
so that's what i think...

### 6 — KyleGoetz

I changed the information about this entry, because the entry appeared to be a grammar rule for the plain past tense of 始める, when in fact the rule can be abstracted to cover the entirety of conjugations of the verb (be it plain, polite, past, non-past).<br/>
<br/>
@Miki - arkofnoah hit it right on the head; 〜出す carries an "explosive" feeling along with it. 〜始める does not.
