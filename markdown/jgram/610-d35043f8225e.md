## Notes

### 1 — Ayumi

Basics<br/>
---<br/>
V-masu:<br/>
-iru/-eru: remove 'ru' and add -masu <br/>
ex: (taberu = tabe-masu; miru = mimasu0<br/>
Everything else: change last syllable to 'i' row<br/>
ex: (yomu = yomimasu; tobu = tobimasu)<br/>
<br/>
V-'te<br/>
-iru/-eru: change 'ru' to 'te'<br/>
ex: shinjiru = shinjite; ikiru = ikite<br/>
-ku: change 'ku' to 'i' and add 'te'<br/>
ex: naku = naite; saku = saite<br/>
-gu: change 'gu' to 'i' and add 'de'<br/>
ex: nugu = nuide; sawagu = sawaide<br/>
-tsu/-ru/-u: change 'tsu'/'ru'/'u' to 't' and add 'te'<br/>
ex: tatsu = tatte; tooru = tootte<br/>
-nu/-mu/-bu: change 'nu'/'mu'/'bu' to 'n' and add 'de'<br/>
-su: change 'su' to 'shi' and add 'te'<br/>
ex: utsusu = utsushite; shimesu = shimeshite<br/>
*There are exeption -iru/-eru that follow the -ru rule:<br/>
ex: hashiru = hashitte; kaeru = kaette<br/>
<br/>
V-nai<br/>
-iru/-eru: remove 'ru' and add 'nai'<br/>
ex: kanjiru = kanjinai; ateru = atenai<br/>
Everything else: change last syllable to 'a' row<br/>
ex: watasu = watasanai, chikayoru = chikayoranai

### 2 — flamewizzy21

Not ALL -/i/ru and -/e/ru verbs are known as /ru/ verbs. (these are verbs that drop the ru at the end)<br/>
Basically, all -ru verbs end with -/i/ru and -/e/ru, but not all -/i/ru and -/e/ru verbs are -ru verbs. An example will clear it up.<br/>
<br/>
Sample -ru verbs:<br/>
tabe<b>ru</b>;　食べ<b>る</b><br/>
hajime<b>ru</b>; 始め<b>る</b><br/>
hai<b>ru</b>; 入<b>る</b><br/>
de<b>ru</b>; 出<b>る</b><br/>
oki<b>ru</b>; 起き<b>る</b><br/>
<br/>
Sample -/u/ verbs that end in -/i/ru or -/e/ru:<br/>
ir<b>u</b>; 要<b>る</b><br/>
kir<b>u</b>; 切<b>る</b><br/>
shaber<b>u</b>; しゃべ<b>る</b><br/>
shir<b>u</b>; 知<b>る</b><br/>
<br/>
These are only examples. 95% of all -/i/ru and -/e/ru verbs are -ru verbs. Take note of the exceptions and you will be fine.

### 4 — Stealthmate1233

Most -eru verbs are Ichidan (-ru) verbs. The are basically 3 exceptions to this which are regularly used, so if you remember them you'll be fine.<br/>
喋<b>る </b>ー＞喋<b>ります </b><br/>
帰<b>る </b>ー＞帰<b>ります </b><br/>
返<b>る </b>ー＞返<b>ります </b><br/>
There are probably more exceptions to this, but as I said, these here are what you hear every day.
