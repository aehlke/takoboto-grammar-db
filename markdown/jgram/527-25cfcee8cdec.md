## Discussion

### 1 — dc

also written as における

### 2 — Miki

The sample is ok.

### 3 — dc

I think this should be deleted as it is the same as <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> じゃないか？

### 4 — anon

They originally consist of ni + oku. Usage are slightly different. So the same entry would be easier to understand the difference.

### 5 — dc

i think this is different from <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>, which can also be shortened to nioite. <br/>
における is at a physical place. This nioite relates more to an abstract place/situation. similar, but different?

### 6 — bamboo4

<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=nioite#">#</a></b> is out of place when compaed with the rest of the examples.

### 7 — danielredgate

How about the translation 'regarding'? This seems to fit most cases better than the suggestions given at the top.

### 8 — SB

Please help me to understand the difference between nioite and niokeru.

### 9 — ashishjoshi

それは私の人生における、最良の日であった。<br/>
会議は第一会議室において、行われる。

### 10 — Apollo\_Lycos

Are "ni oite" and "ni totte" synonymous? It seems like there are instances where the meanings overlap.

### 11 — DrJones

Ni oite = in (this context/ubication) ~<br/>
Ni totte = for (this subgroup of people) X applies.
