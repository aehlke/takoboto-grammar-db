## Meaning

because,  as expected (used for positive things)

the food was good as expected

## Notes

### 1 — dc

This phrase needs a cause and effect. <br/>
"cause <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> effect"<br/>
"He studied hard <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> passed the test"<br/>
<br/>
You cannot use just the result<br/>
NG: "he <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> passed the test"<br/>
<br/>
Very similiar to <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> but that word can be used on its own a little more casually, as in<br/>
"sasuga Rob-san" = isn't Rob great, after all.<br/>
<br/>
Works also for negative nuance, as in the "and got fat" example below.

### 2 — rubyhatchet

だけあって is used for good merits. It should not be used for negative nuances. However, だけに can be used for both good and bad merits.<br/>
<br/>
Often, さすが(に) is used in conjunction with だけあって as emphasis.
