## Meaning

no mistake...definitely 確かに　と　おもう

I've not got my keys, I definitely (lit. make no mistake) I dropped them somewhere

## Notes

### 1 — dc

Noun + に違いない<br/>
ダイヤに違いない<br/>
<br/>
Verb-plain + に違いない<br/>
殺したに違いない

### 2 — ved

ex-かぎガないどこかに落としたに違いない

### 3 — Tetsuzan

Similar to the usage of に決まっている、に違いない is simply translated most of the time as "definitely" or "absolutely".  One problem with this is that the usage of both can be a little bit ambiguous and can leave a serious student up in the air of which to use.<br/>
<br/>
A less spoken concept regarding any grammar involves the context and "feeling" that is generally understood by native Japanese, but may be difficult for foreign learners to grasp.  <br/>
<br/>
に決まっている has the connotation of narrowness of options such that whatever precedes this is an absolute.<br/>
<br/>
 例文）　地球は平らではないに決まっている。　<br/>
         The earth is *absolutely* not flat.<br/>
<br/>
In contrast, usage of 違いない　supposedly has a feeling of slight openness in terms of speculation or conditions.<br/>
<br/>
例文）　死体に丸い傷があるので、拳銃でやられちゃったに違いない。<br/>
　　　　Since the corpse has circular wounds, he must have been done in by a handgun.
