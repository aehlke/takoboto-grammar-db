## Meaning

not only, but also  starting with

not only french, but also german, spanish etc...

## Notes

### 1 — dc

&lt;b&gt;を始め&lt;/b&gt; is used to preface a list of items, and also to emphasize the first one.<br/>
<br/>
A をはじめ、B, C ...<br/>
<br/>
English equivalents might be &lt;b&gt;not only, but also&lt;/b&gt; <br/>
Not only A, but also B and C<br/>
Especially A, but also B and C<br/>
<br/>
similar to <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> which is mostly used for negative things.<br/>
<br/>
かれの下品の言い方&lt;b&gt;からして&lt;/b&gt;、すごく嫌な人です<br/>
I really dislike him, &lt;b&gt;especially&lt;/b&gt; because of his foul mouth.

## Discussion

### 1 — dc

used when giving a list of things, the first example being X

### 2 — Amatuka

を is used to mark a noun or noun phrase.<br/>
<br/>
N = noun.

### 3 — dc

This puts emphasis on the first item strongly<br/>
"First and foremost X, and also ... "

### 4 — dc

can this be used with a verb?<br/>
　歌えるを始め、踊りました<br/>
　first singing, but also dancing

### 5 — Miki

It is weird to be used with a verb. I cannot think of any good eamples using a verb using 始め.<br/>
&gt;&gt;歌うことから始め、踊りもした。

### 6 — dc

&gt; 歌うことから始め &lt;<br/>
so that is turning the verb into a noun.. <br/>
after singing , dancing...

### 7 — bamboo4

を始め does not necessairly mean "to start with" but the means of enumeration.　彼は英語を始め、フランス語とロシア語にも堪能だ only serves as the means of enumeration. "He is conversant not only in English but also in French and Russian." <br/>

### 8 — dc

but it might also mean especially, as in this example:<br/>
<br/>
田中先生をはじめ、この学校の皆さんのお世話になりました。<br/>
（たなかせんせいをはじめ、このがっこうのみなさんのおせわになりました。）<br/>
I Have been helped by everyone in  this school, but especially by Mr. Tanaka.

### 9 — dc

but it might also mean <b>especially</b>, as in this example:<br/>
