## Meaning

Is (animate)

There's a dog in that car.

## Notes

### 1 — Rinji\_HalfElf

<b><b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b></b><br/>
<br/>
Like <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> iru is also an stative (non-action) verb. Iru, unlike aru is used to express "is" or "exists" for living things, people, or beings, with plants being excluded.<br/>
Like <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>, iru uses <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>/に to indicate the location when specified.<br/>
<br/>
<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> also is used as an auxillary verb to form progressive tenses. Similar in function to English's verb "to be" in the following: He <b>is</b> going, I <b>was</b> going, We <b>are</b> going, etc.<br/>
<br/>
<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> does not require the use of <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>/が.<br/>
<br/>
When <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> is used in an auxillary form, it can be used with non-living things just like <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>.<br/>
<br/>
For an auxillary form, <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> is usually preceeded by the -te form of the other verb. Then iru is conjugated accordingly.<br/>
<br/>
<br/>
Aruite imasu. --&gt; (He/she,you/it/etc.) is walking.<br/>
Aruite imashita. (He/she,you/it/etc.) was walking.<br/>
<br/>
Note that iru cannot be used for the future tense, as there isn't a real future tense in Japan. Therefore, sentences like "He will be going." will not exist.<br/>
<br/>
<b>Other notes</b><br/>
<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> is commonly written with kana alone, instead of kanji. Kanji can be used in written Japanese to indicate subtle nuances in meaning.

## Discussion

### 1 — Amatuka

'iru' for _need_ has a) a different kanji and b) is godan not ichidan.<br/>
"kare wa iranai" "I don't need him(boyfriends)"<br/>
"kare wa inai" "He's not here(I don't have a boyfriend)"<br/>

### 2 — your name

I remember that "iru" is for living things because it is used in the construction te+iru for the present progressive tense. And it is usually a living creature that is presently doing something. This isn't always true I guess, but it helps me remember the difference.

### 3 — Amatuka

ている <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> can't be used with the verbs of existance (e.g. ある <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> and いる <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>)

### 4 — Sasuke21

I thought you can say あっている when talking about past experience, as in: <br/>
Ａさん：私の息子にもう会ったことがあるはずだよね<br/>
Ｂさん：確かじゃないけど、あってると思う。<br/>
A：I expect you have already met my son, right?<br/>
B: I'm not sure, but I think I have. <br/>
please correct me if I'm wrong because I have been wondering about this! ^-^

### 5 — bamboo4

あっている is past of 会う＋いる and いる in this sense denotes the continuation of some status or event, here the fact that you have met and that situaiton continues to exist.

### 6 — zeptimius

My two Japanese (native) teachers say that iru applies only to animal life, or more specifically, as they say, 'something that has a heart'. So for trees, flowers etc. you use aru. Also, how about animals without hearts? I think those exist, like jellyfish or something.
