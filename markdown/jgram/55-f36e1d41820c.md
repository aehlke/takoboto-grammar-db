## Meaning

thanks to

It's thanks to you that I received the promotion.

## Notes

### 1 — lucinda lohmanoota

Used to show causation for something good.

## Discussion

### 1 — Amatuka

### 2 — Miki

hither and thither あちらこちらに<br/>

### 3 — bamboo4

The second example is to show that this expression can be used in a reverse situation.

### 4 — PaulO

Hang on bamboo4,<br/>
<br/>
In all of the textbooks I've read, お陰で can only be used to indicate a positive outcome in JLPT2. It may be used in other contexts (maybe to express irony in colloquial Japanese) but for JLPT2, I don't think your example is valid.<br/>
<br/>
I think you mean 〜のせいで.

### 5 — bamboo4

On the contrary, お陰で can be used in a negative sense in many situations, e.g., お前さんのお陰で大変な損をしたよ.<br/>
<br/>
I don't personally care a hoot about JLPT or whatever - when you have to learn, you have to learn.<br/>

### 6 — tanuki86

PaulO is correct. おかげで is used in positive connotations, such as "thanks to...".<br/>
<br/>
While せいで is used in negative connotations, usually to express blame, such as "because of...".<br/>
<br/>
And I agree that these examples should be groomed according to grammar level. However, exceptions can be noted if the context/situation is properly explained.

### 7 — laki-laura

普通はよい結果になる場合に使うが、皮肉な表現として、悪い結果になる場合にも使うことがある。 This is written in my JLPT2 book, so bamboo4 is right.<br/>

### 8 — tigert

I have heard it used in negative situations as well, although this site is for JLPT preperation. the ultimate goal should be to learn Japanese. <br/>
<br/>
Classroom Japanese is far from the real thing. keep that in mind while you study and you might improve faster than you had expected.
