## Meaning

~?,  like か

(What) is it, again?

## Notes

### 1 — rubyhatchet

This grammar is used only in conversation. It is used when asking a question in order to confirm/affirm something.<br/>
<br/>
＊Because it an expression for confirmation, it is alright to use past tense when referring to something in the present or future.<br/>
EX: 来月の三日は月曜日でしたっけ。(＝月曜日だったっけ)<br/>
"The 3rd of next month was Monday, right?"<br/>
<br/>
FORMATION:<br/>
V(plain past form) + っけ<br/>
V(dict. form) + んだっけ・んだったっけ<br/>
いA(minus い) + かったっけ<br/>
なA + だ・だった + っけ<br/>
N + だ・だった + っけ<br/>
<br/>
＊If you wish to use more formal Japanese, you can use 「ましたっけ」 or 「でしたっけ」, but you CANNOT use 「ますっけ」 or 「ですっけ」.

### 2 — 反戦

っけ is used as a question marker in informal Speech (and even then is often just shortened to け。)<br/>
<br/>
っけ itself is generally used when asking about something you should really already know but have forgotten, or when you are affirming something that you're maybe 50% sure about.

## Discussion

### 1 — Amatuka

け (usually っけ) is used when asking about something you _should already know_ (e.g. because you've been told but since forgotten/become unsure).

### 2 — dc

  nan deshita-kke? <br/>
  ah, you know, whatchermecallit

yes, so you can also say 誰だったかなぁ。誰だったかしら。-かしら is used mainly by female.

### 5 — wdelossantos

does 来た now mean "went"? (ex: <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=kke#">#</a></b>)

### 7 — KyleGoetz

っけ may _only_ follow the あ-row of kana. Thus, だっけ and ましたっけ are correct, but してっけ or 食べるっけ, etc., are incorrect.
