a thing that, the thing

## Discussion

### 1 — Amatuka

This is an example where the English is forced - but the Japanese quite natural.  It's difficult to get the nuance across.

### 2 — Amatuka

Sort of "To be a company is to be [at risk of going] bankrupt."

### 3 — Amatuka

Not sure the example I've just added goes with the other one.  まあ、いいだろう。

### 4 — blabby

How about "Of course", "its obvious", "everyone knows", "naturally".  I think it can be used to say that it is something that is always so. (一般的なこと)

### 5 — 誠

It shouldn't be translated as " a thing" since もの（thing) and ものだ are different, the latter is a grammar. 〜ものだ has different meanings and one of it is 〜が一般的な常識だ as what bladdy さん said.  Translated as " It is known that..." ,  "It is common sense that ...." , "It is commonly known that...." , "It is generally known that...."<br/>

### 6 — 誠

ex <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=monoda#">#</a></b>  (As we know/As it is generally known), human life is but ephemeral

### 7 — 誠

ex <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=monoda#">#</a></b> " (Generally known), companies do have the possiblity of facing bankruptcy". <br/>
Where here I treat the sentence as 会社は潰れる　＋　ものだ. But if you want to say " The company is a thing that can face bankruptcy" then you are treating it as 会社は＋潰れるものだ which is a dfferent meaning. <br/>
So ex<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=monoda#">#</a></b> isn't a good example and I think can be safely removed. 〜ものだ usually used in advising or reminding someone of something of a common sense and a sense of "should"ness or obligation . <br/>
"It is common sense that (one should)...."

### 8 — nay

Oh uhm.. no.<br/>
Please don't confuse this construction with an other use of mono da.(-&gt; monoda-2)<br/>
<br/>
While this one here is used to create a sentence structure which represents a characteristic of the subject instead of describing the actions taken or received by the subject (change of sentence pattern) the latter may be used to express something as common sense [it is (generally) known/common sense that ...(should)"	(also an ideal or one’s conviction)] with an emotional nuance.<br/>
<br/>
So actually I'd say that example <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=monoda#">#</a></b> is the one to show this grammars use most obviously. <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=monoda#">#</a></b>, however, should be moved to "monoda-2".<br/>
<br/>
But as mono da has so many meanings it's hard to differenciate. Though one..<br/>
