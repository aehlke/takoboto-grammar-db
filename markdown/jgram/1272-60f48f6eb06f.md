## Discussion

### 1 — Amatuka

The example is actually 'doori'. 予想通り 【よそうどおり】 (exp) as expected (neutral and negative events); as feared

### 2 — Amatuka

Many '通り' 'toori' examples are actually a literal 'way' as in street.

### 3 — Miki

私が言った通りになったでしょう！<br/>
watashi ga itta _toori_ ni natta deshou!

### 4 — Amatuka

Formed from (plain) verb + toori or<br/>
noun + toori.<br/>
Note that a euphonic change to doori is possible depending on what it follows.<br/>
<br/>
V = Verb<br/>
N = Noun

### 5 — dc

when does it have に after?<br/>
私が教えた通り<b>に</b>、、

### 6 — Miki

You can also say <br/>
予想 通りに 日本チームが優勝した。<br/>
彼女は もと 通りに 元気になった。<br/>

I have always thought of　<br/>
「〜 通りに」　as ”like 〜”<br/>
<br/>

「〜通り」as "same way as 〜" <br/>
<br/>

<br/>
the meaning doesn`t change much, but thinking of it like this makes translation less complicated. <br/>
