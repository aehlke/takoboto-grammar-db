## Meaning

beside

the king sat beside the queen

## Notes

### 1 — ninja\_k

〜のかたわら can also be in the more figurative sense as well. It is also a more archaic expression in spoken Japanese (according to a friend)<br/>

### 4 — blabby

This is in my 一級 grammar book.  The explanation is "〜しながら、同時に"<br/>
This is different to 傍ら/側ら/かたわら(で/に) "Next to/Nearby" as bamboo4 said.

### 5 — blabby

An example of the 一級 grammar is<br/>
私は会社に勤めてるかたわら、夜は家で日本語を勉強しています。<br/>
Besides working, at night I study Japanese at home.

### 6 — crlane

平日に仕事をするかたわら、休みの日にはボランティアをしている。<br/>
Besides working during the week, (he/she/they)do/does volunteer work on (his/her/their) days off.<br/>
<br/>
You could also replace "besides" with "while".  かたわら is used figuratively and not.  <br/>
When used after a verb it usually means 〜しながら、同時に<br/>
When used after a noun it usually means "to be physicaly beside".<br/>
かたわらに置いています　"placed beside"<br/>
〜のかたわらに "beside 〜" <br/>
There should most definatley be another meaning and example added to the entry.
