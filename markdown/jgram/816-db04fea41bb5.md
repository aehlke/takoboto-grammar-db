## Meaning

just at the time when

just as the doors opened

## Examples

### 1179 — dc

<a href="http://www.csse.monash.edu.au/~jwb/cgi-bin/wwwjdic.cgi?9MHJ電車のドアが開いたかどうかのうちに">
				電車のドアが開いた<strong>かどうかのうちに</strong><strong></strong></a> <br/>
<strong>Before you know </strong>
		if the train doors opened or not.

### 5243 — eastmael

<a href="http://www.csse.monash.edu.au/~jwb/cgi-bin/wwwjdic.cgi?9MHJ授業ベルが鳴らすかどうか教室から学生が出ました。">
授業ベルが<strong>鳴らすかどうか</strong>教室から学生が出ました。<strong></strong></a> <br/>
<strong>Just as the school bell rang, </strong>
		the students dashed out of the classroom.

## Discussion

### 1 — dc

this is almost as if the user is not sure the new state has occurred yet.. did the doors open ("ka dou ka") or not yet..

### 2 — bamboo4

Literally, it means "while it is not certain whether the door opened or not," so you cannot tell if the door opened or not. It depends upon the text that follows, but normally, it would mean that the door did open.<br/>

### 3 — Miki

<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE=kadoukanouchini#">#</a></b> 授業（終了の）ベルが鳴るかどうかのうちに　is better. <br/>
I would say from the English...<br/>
授業終了のベルが鳴るか鳴らないかのうちに、学生たちは教室から飛び出しました。  This does not mean students flew out, by the way.

### 4 — sagittarius

actually, if you said,  'the students flew out' that would be acceptable english. it is understood that you mean that they moved quickly, not that they flew like birds.

### 5 — claytonian

are we sure this is a real and grammatically correct phrase?  Google doesn't have many hits for it. Maybe a note about how it is a replacement for another phrase...
