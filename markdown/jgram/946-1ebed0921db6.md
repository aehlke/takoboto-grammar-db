## Meaning

suddenly,  by coincidence

without thinking, I shouted out

## Notes

### 1 — dc

<b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> and <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> do not always have negative connotations <br/>
<br/>
whereas <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> implies more regret, or that the action was done carelessly.

### 2 — dc

other words for suddenly include <br/>
　いきなり<br/>
　突然「とつぜん」

## Discussion

### 1 — dc

whats the difference with いきなり or 突然？

### 2 — Miki

hmm this is a very good question... Let me think.

### 3 — bamboo4

ふと would be used in  the sense of "incidentally" or "by some unexpected turn of event." It is more or less an unexpected thing that catch you more or less passively.<br/>
<br/>
いきなり and 突然 has the connotation of "out of blue" or "abruptly" so that there is a big element of surprise.

### 4 — marvin

what is the difference with 偶然 ?

### 5 — dc

偶然 「ぐうぜん」 I think means more what a coincidence. As in "what a surprise to see you here!"<br/>
<br/>
突然　「とつぜん」is more similar, like "out of the blue".<br/>
<br/>
いきなり is another similar word. <br/>
いきなり結婚した - got married all of a sudden

### 6 — Kaens

For me, there are hints (like endling と) for the word is an onomatopoeia construction, that's the difference... As in, 「ふと気がついたの。」"'Pooh!' - the realization came." Although it's not as childish in Japanese, as they use onomatopoeia words a lot.

### 7 — christ

### 8 — ookaminote

Kaens へ<br/>
