## Meaning

One's intention; conviction; belief その意志

I intend to do that; I am convinced that ...

## Notes

### 1 — 赤毛

Two ways to inform the interlocutor of the wishes of the speaker <br/>
(different in Japanese from the way to tell about the wishes of a third person):<br/>
1) Verb in volitional form (意向形) + と + 思う: <br/>
jp: 一万円ほど父に都合してもらおう<strong>と思う。<br/>
en: I think I will ask my father to lend me 10000 yen.<br/>
2) Verb in plain form + noun to mark intention + だ or です or である<br/>
The noun can be 考え（かんがえ）,予定（よてい）, or 積もり（つもり）which is the more common.<br/>
This last way is easier, for example to say you don't want to do something. The negative form of つもりだ is つもりはない (and not つもりではない).<br/>
<br/>
Be careful, the use of つもり after た形 have a different meaning <br/>
(see entry <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> and <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b>).</strong>

### 2 — Campbell

つもり does not really mean intention.  American textbooks and instructors simply explain it as such because it is simpler.  つもり really describes a state of mind.  行くつもりだ it is the state of mind to go = I intend to go.  what about: 行ったつもりだ?  in english it doesn't make sense to say: I intend to went...  it is I am in the frame of mind as if I had gone...  if that makes any sense...<br/>
<br/>
よく読んだつもりです。I am convinced I read it carefully.<br/>
一所懸命やったつもりで。　I believe I did my best.<br/>
(Examples provided from Making Sense of Japanese: What the Textbooks don't tell you by Jay Rubin)

### 4 — tigert

The thing about つもり is that using it gives the listener the impression that you won`t complete the action you intend on doing. From the people that I have talked with there are levels of commitment in the japanese language. the first level is negative statement. which is avoided at all cost.<br/>
EX No, I won`t go.<br/>
<br/>
then comes  passive nagative statement.<br/>
<br/>
EX I would like to go, but ....(I`m not going)<br/>
<br/>
then comes probablility related statements. which have their own level.<br/>
行くかも知らない I don`t know if I`ll go,(but I probably won`t)<br/>
行くつもり　I intend to go (but I won`t go because well you know how it is)<br/>
多分行く　I think I will go ( unless something comes up.) <br/>
<br/>
and the final stage is the affirmative statement.<br/>
行く　I　will go.<br/>
<br/>

つもり should be better described as an adverb of certainty, being VERY CERTAIN.<br/>
<br/>

<br/>
彼女はきれいなつもりだ<br/>
She is really good looking, isn't she (or, "She's hot!")<br/>

### 11 — Armadillomon

Campbell is right. 行ったつもりだ does NOT mean "I intended to go". That would be 行くつもりだった.<br/>
行ったつもりだ means "I'm sure I went" or "I'm convinced I went".
