## Meaning

indicates direction of verb. Also pronounced 'e'

I will go abroad next year.

## Notes

### 1 — Sakuratree

へ and に there is no difference. They can both be used and are considered interchangeable. I see both に and へ in writing and talking. <br/>
<br/>
Such as:<br/>
<br/>
えきにいきます。- I'm going to the train station.<br/>
<br/>
 へ can replace に and vice versa.

### 3 — Amatuka

"In motion constructions -ni and -e appear to have the same distribution [...]. The primary difference in distribution is that -ni may occur in other verbal contexts, as I show below, but -e may not, while -e may appear marking arguments in nominal constructions but -ni may not, [...]"

### 4 — Amatuka

In <b>theory</b> へ is for movement in a direction, while に implies arrival at a specific destination.<br/>
[or some such].  In practise there is very little difference.

### 5 — Amatuka

N = noun

### 6 — Amatuka

<b>Note:</b> Apart from the <br/>
DCへ <br/>
letter / email addressing type usage you can't use へ to indicate going to a person.<br/>
e.g. (BAD) DCへ行きました。[DC he ikimashita] I went to DC.<br/>
(GOOD) DCの家へ行きました。[DC no ie he ikimashita] I went to DC's house.
