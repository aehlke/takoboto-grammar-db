## Meaning

without

i went without eating

## Notes

### 1 — sw

Equivalent to <b><a href="http://jgram.org/pages/viewOne.php?tagE="></a></b> and is often used in writing.

### 2 — maki

formation: take the negative root (ない形）and replace the final ~ない　with ~ず<br/>
<br/>
例）<br/>
<br/>
　　書く　-&gt; 書か「ない」 -&gt; 書かず（に）<br/>
　　見る　-&gt;　 見「ない」 -&gt; 見ず<br/>
　　来る　-&gt; 来ず　（読み方：こず）<br/>
　　する　-&gt; せず　　<br/>
<br/>
Compared to the "naide" form, this one is used with a more negative connection to the thing you didn't do (as you would regret that you didn't do something). <br/>
<br/>
例）<br/>
<br/>
昨日学校へ行かず、ゲームセンターで遊んでしまった。

you can also shorten 〜ずに into 〜ず.<br/>

### 4 — Watari

聞き返すことも忘れずに！<br/>
Don't forget to ask in return. (Example: "How are you?"  "Fine, thanks. And you?")<br/>
This is from a Japanese English teacher's lesson.

### 5 — K

〜ずに is the shortened form of 〜ないで

### 6 — shiho-chan

Isnt 〜ずに　A more formal form, not a contraction?

### 7 — sw

I think ず is a classical form of ない that still comes up, as in 知らず.  〜ずに means 〜ないで, and is used mostly in writting, although I heard it spoken sometimes.

### 8 — bamboo4

It may of interest to note that in some dialect, 行かず would mean 行こう(let's go).<br/>

### 9 — ngonhan2k7

### 10 — nellyaudrey

how do you say the negative version of 食べず　is it 食べずじゃない? just for the purpose of saying something like, i didn't come to school without eating. 学校に食べずじゃないで来た．or would you just neg kuru? like, 学校を食べずに来なかった．

### 11 — RBY

To nellyaudrey<br/>
You can' t do a negative of a negative form in Japanese. You'll only be mishunderstood. The Japanese are more simple minded. The negative of a negative is positive, so say it positive! 朝ご飯食べて学校に来ました。I came to school having eaten breakfast.<br/>
Anyway, if you want to say your negative-negative sentence at any cost the closer sentence you can use is a negation of the all positive sentence. 朝ご飯食べずに学校に来ましたと違います.It's not true that I came to school without eating breakfast.
