## Meaning

that Both あの and その can be used with the meaning ‘that ~’ when an item you are referring to is not in sight. あの is used when both the speaker and the hearer are (or are assumed to be) familiar with a person or thing in question.

あの人

## Notes

### 1 — Rushyne

Both あの and その can be used with the meaning ‘that ~’ when an item you are referring to is not in sight. あの is used when both the speaker and the hearer are (or are assumed to be) familiar with a person or thing in question.

### 2 — jeina419

ex: ano kanojou no namae wa yumi da.<br/>
trans: that girl's name is yumi.

### 3 — Sakuratree

あの (ano) and その (sono) are similar. However, ano is used for objects- people, places...and same with sono.<br/>
<br/>
Ano however is for a thing far away from both the speaker and listener.<br/>
<br/>
Meanwhile, sono is for something close to the listener but not close to the speaker.

### 5 — KWhazit

In response to walter's questions:<br/>
-There are several ways of Romanizing Japanese. Depending on the system you use, かのじょ could be either 'kanojo' or 'kanojyo'. Miki's point is that it's not 'kanojou' because there's no う on the end.<br/>
-As for the second question...<br/>
...first off, you can use お父さん to talk about other people's fathers:<br/>
みちこのお父さんは親切です。 - Michiko's father is kind.<br/>
...and secondly, if you're being formal (which the example seems to be), you'd normally us 父 (ちち) to refer to your own father. But that's not stopping anyone in more casual speech, though I think there's a tendency to drop the お in that case:<br/>
父さんに部屋を掃除させられちゃった…<br/>
I got forced to clean my room by my dad...
