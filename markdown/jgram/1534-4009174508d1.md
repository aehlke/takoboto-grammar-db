## Notes

### 1 — jeep

Another way to say "big" in Japanese is "dekkai," as in "dekkai yatsu datta (he was huge)"

### 2 — deviruchi

The na adjective version of ookii can only be used with abstract concepts.<br/>
i.e. ookina mondai (a big problem).

### 3 — tigert

Adjectives are seperated into 3 groups, much the same way that verbs are. these groups are<br/>
い-Adjectives, な-Adjectives, and irregular Adjectives. <br/>
<br/>
いーadjectives are the easiest to spot because the end with い<br/>
examples<br/>
おいし&lt;font color=blue&gt;い&lt;/font&gt;<br/>
大き&lt;font color=blue&gt;い&lt;/font&gt;<br/>
小さ&lt;font color=blue&gt;い&lt;/font&gt;<br/>
<br/>
な-adjectives are all adjectives that don`t end with い, they are called な- adjectives because when you attach them to nouns you insert a な　at the end of the adjective.<br/>
example<br/>
親切&lt;font color=red size=1&gt;な&lt;/font&gt;ひと<br/>
at the moment I can@t think of any other examples but trust me there are more than a few な　adjectives. if you see two kanji together then chances are it is a な adjective. <br/>
<br/>
the final group is the Irregulare adjectives. As I`m sure you have guessed they get their name because they don`t follow the rules of the other two groups. Irregulare adjectives are adjectives that end in い　but when attached to a noun follow the rules of the な　adjectives.<br/>
The best example of this is <br/>
きれ&lt;font color=blue&gt;い&lt;/font&gt;&lt;font color=red&gt;な&lt;/font&gt;女<br/>
<br/>
irregulare adjectives are the least common the the three groups.<br/>
<br/>
Using な with い-Adjectives<br/>
it is true that you can treat most いadjectives as な adjectives by replacing the final い　with な<br/>
大きな問題<br/>
小さな間違い<br/>
<br/>
but as far as I know this can only be used with abstract concepts. I have heard it used to discribe objects, but only when the object was a wish or desire
