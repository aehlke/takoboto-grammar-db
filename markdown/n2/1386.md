# 邪魔する

Source: [Takoboto #1386](https://takoboto.jp/bunpo/1386/)  
Label: jamasuru  
JLPT: N2  
Credits: dc  
Retrieved: 2026-10-03T05:39:09+00:00  
License: [CC BY-SA 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.0/)

Other forms: じゃまする

## Meaning

to interrupt, to disturb

I don't want to disturb you

Meaning credits: dc

## Examples

### Example 6526

彼のいびきが一晩中邪魔になった

かれのいびきがいちばんちゅうじゃまになった

en: His snoring disturbed me all night.

Credits: ラブレス

### Example 6527

あの子はいつも付きまとって邪魔だ

あのこはいつもつきまとってじゃまだ

en: That child is always underfoot.

Credits: ラブレス

それが彼の成功の邪魔になる

それがかれのせいこうのじゃまになる

en: It will become an obstacle in his success.

## Discussion

### Comment 1 — dc

I often wonder how to interrupt people to ask them a question at work...<br/>----<br/><br/>すみません、少しよろしいですか？ might be short and polite enough.<br/><br/>One way to sound polite or reserved is try not to finish your sentence.<br/>It might sound weird but a half-finished sense can hand over the baton to your partner.<br/>For instance, ”すみません、質問があるんですが、、、。”<br/>It lets the partner take over the conversation, he may say, "何ですか？”then you can proceed saying, "質問があるんですけど”.<br/>Your sentence ”すみません、質問ありますので、少し時間いただけますか？"has no problem but it can be a too long sentence depending on the scene.<br/>In other words you might end up with holding your partner from speaking for too long.<br/>Your sentence might be better to finish halfway such as ”すみません、質問があるんですけど…” or finish it by combining all the elements, ”すみません、質問してもよろしいですか？”

### Comment 2 — tigert

I have found that in working situations 失礼しますが is always a good way to interupt someone while they are in the middle of something. <br/><br/>if someone is eating you can always say すみません食事中のに よろしいですか。

### Comment 3 — Eric

I think a simple ちょっといいですか (chotto ii desu ka), or XX さん、今、時間大丈夫ですか (XX-san, ima, jikan daijoubu desu ka?) is fine for many situations. In general, the workplace is an informal situation when dealing with colleagues. Superiors are a different matter, and would call for honorifics and other bells and whistles.
