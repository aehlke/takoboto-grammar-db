## Meaning

without, leaving out

Without a strong will, it's not possible for this work to be finished.

ja: ふつうはふくまれるもの、当然あるものを加えずに

Meaning credits: ziaz

## Examples

### Example 6070

強い意志**を抜きにして**は、この仕事を完成させることはできない。

つよいいしをぬきにしては、このしごとをかんせいさせることはできない。

en: **Without a** strong will, it's not possible for this work to be finished.

Credits: ziaz

### Example 8080

皆さん、仕事の話は抜きにして楽しく飲みましょう。

みなさん、しごとのはなしはぬきにしてたのしくのみましょう。

en: Everyone, let's enjoy drinking without talking about work.

Credits: expandover, Noel

### Example 8081

皆さん、仕事の話は抜きにして楽しく飲みましょう。

みなさん、しごとのはなしはぬきにしてたのしくのみましょう。

en: Everyone, without talking about work and let's enjoy drinking.

### Example 7113

料理の上手な山田さん**をぬきにして**は、パーティーは開けません。

りょうりのじょうずなやまださんをぬきにしては、パーティーはあけません。

en: **Without Mr Yamada,** who is a really good cook, we can't have a party.

### Comment 1 — IMABI

[wo nuki ni shite/wa nuki ni shite] can have two generally the same usages. It can be used to show that something is without, or left out. Wa does not have to follow after shite but it does give extra emphasis as [wa] is frequently called the highlighting particle. [nuki] itself can be used to follow a noun directly to mean "without" especially when listing ingredients or talking about doing something "without". [Ni shite] itself gives meaning to nuki showing that it is "for" whatever [nuki] is excluding that causes/does such and such. So [nuki ni shite wa]could be looked at as meaning for without...<br/>I would also like to mention that a lot of people need to watch their Romanization on this site as somethings are just wrong. This page should be wo nuki ni shite/ wa naku ni shite not ha, wa is just a spelling rule since the Heian period to be written with the Kana character for ha.
