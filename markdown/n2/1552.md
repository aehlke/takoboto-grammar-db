# に違いない

Source: [Takoboto #1552](https://takoboto.jp/bunpo/1552/)

Credits: alexeiS

Other forms: にちがいない

## Meaning

no mistake...definitely

I've not got my keys, I definitely (lit. make no mistake) I dropped them somewhere

ja: 確かに　と　おもう

Meaning credits: alexeiS

### Comment 3 — Tetsuzan

Similar to the usage of に決まっている、に違いない is simply translated most of the time as "definitely" or "absolutely". One problem with this is that the usage of both can be a little bit ambiguous and can leave a serious student up in the air of which to use.<br/><br/>A less spoken concept regarding any grammar involves the context and "feeling" that is generally understood by native Japanese, but may be difficult for foreign learners to grasp. <br/><br/>に決まっている has the connotation of narrowness of options such that whatever precedes this is an absolute.<br/><br/> 例文）　地球は平らではないに決まっている。　<br/> The earth is *absolutely* not flat.<br/><br/>In contrast, usage of 違いない　supposedly has a feeling of slight openness in terms of speculation or conditions.<br/><br/>例文）　死体に丸い傷があるので、拳銃でやられちゃったに違いない。<br/> Since the corpse has circular wounds, he must have been done in by a handgun.
