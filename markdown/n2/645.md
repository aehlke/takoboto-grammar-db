# に伴って

Source: [Takoboto #645](https://takoboto.jp/bunpo/645/)  
Label: nitomonatte  
JLPT: N2  
Credits: dc  
Retrieved: 2026-10-03T05:37:49+00:00

Other forms: にともなって

## Meaning

as; in proportion to; with

The number of problems related to the sale of pets has been increasing with the recent pet boom.

ja: とともに; につれて; に従って

Meaning credits: dc

## Formation

- Verb (dictionary form) + に伴って
- Noun + に伴って

### Comment 1 — rubyhatchet

＊When と共に(とともに) means 'as,' it can be rephrased as につれて, に伴って(にともなって), or に従って(にしたがって).<br/>EXAMPLE: 景気の回復[と共に/につれて/に伴って/に従って]失業率が下がってきた。(As business recovers, the unemployment rate is coming down.)<br/><br/>FORMATION:<br/>Ｖ(dict. form) + に伴って・に伴い<br/>Ｖ(dict. form) + に伴う + Ｎ<br/>Ｎ + に伴って・に伴い<br/>Ｎ1 + に伴う + Ｎ2 ("accompany" is often a plausible translation)

### Comment 2 — srobertson

### Comment 12 — bamboo4

I would vote for: "Earthquakes are often accompanied by outbreaks of fire." に伴ってdoes not have the meaning of "as a result of" or "as the consequence of" even though it is broad enough to encompass such situation in volving causality. It can be an accidental, as opposed to causal, happenstance.

### Comment 13 — Amatuka

Hmm, however it does have an strong 'direction' and at least a hint of an /implication/ of causality.<br/>Take the statement "Psychosis is often associated with canabis use." vs. "Canabis use is often associated with psychosis."<br/>I think there's a fairly clear difference in implied meaning between the two.

### Comment 15 — bamboo4

As indicated before, 伴って does not have the direct causal relationship with the event in which the secondary event occurs. There may be such causal relationship or there may not be, so that the expression does not exclude causality but that's not the only meaning that you get out of 伴って.
