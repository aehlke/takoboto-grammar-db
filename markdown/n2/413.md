## Meaning

just as I'd, right at the moment when I'd

just as I thought to call her, she called me

## Examples

### Example 368

マニュアルの説明通りにやってみ**たところ**うまくいった。

マニュアルのせつめいどおりにやってみたところうまくいった。

en: **When** I tried doing it as said in the manual, it went well.

Credits: Amatuka

### Example 369

彼女に電話をかけようと考えていた**ところ**、彼女の方から、電話をかけて来た

かのじょにでんわをかけようとかんがえていたところ、かのじょのほうから、でんわをかけてきた

en: **Just when** I'd thought I'd call her, she called me.

Credits: Amatuka

en: **When** questioned whether they used the Internet at home 28.6% said they "currently use it".

Credits: Amatuka

### Comment 1 — rubyhatchet

「Aたところ、Bた」: B is the subsequent result or acquired knowledge, et cetera, after doing A. This is a one-time-only action &amp; result.<br/><br/>Vた + ところ<br/>* the most common use is 〜てみたところ

### Comment 7 — bamboo4

I would say that ところで more or less has location-orientation whereas ところを would have temporal-orientation. Still contrary construction is possible for both of these phrases. I would wager that most of the authors most probably would not have used either of these phrases with any degree of conscious effort to have it meant either way.

### Comment 8 — Amatuka

＾たところ implies you'd just done (e.g. past tense) something. See also ^るところ for just as I was about to.

### Comment 9 — Amatuka

Don't forget that V-たところ can be a literal place where V-た took place. e.g. パーティーをやったところはどこだっけ。'Where was it you had the party again?'

### Comment 14 — 誠

1.volitional form + としたところ＝to be on the point of...／going to/about to/on the verge of (doing something) but just before doing<br/> <br/> Has a similar meaning to 〜(す)るところ＝今からすぐする（まだしていない）<br/>E.g. : 引っ越そうとしたところです。<br/>　　　　I am on the point of moving.<br/> これから、説明するところです。<br/>　　　　From now on, I am going to explain.<br/><br/>２．〜(し)ているところ＝just started doing 〜<br/>今ちょうどしている（まだ終わっていない）<br/>E.g. 今、お風呂に入っているところだから、後で電話　　　してください。<br/>　　　Now I am just having my bath, so please <br/> phone me later.<br/><br/>3. 〜たところ＝just finished 〜ing<br/> ＝今終わったばかり <br/>E.g. : 病気が治ったところなので、まだ泳ぐことができない。<br/>　　　Since he had just recovered from the illness, therefore he still can't swim.<br/><br/>４．Another very similar meaning of 〜たところ＝<br/>　　〜たらその結果＝once (he/she) had 〜<br/>E.g. : 電話で問い合わせたところ、休館日は月曜日だということです。<br/>　　Once I had enquired by phone, I was told that the off day is Monday.<br/><br/>5. 〜ている／ていたところに＝just at that moment, one is/was/were about to(present / past tense depends on the sentence)<br/> 　also under 〜ところに／へ／を＝〜という状況、場面に／へ／を<br/><br/>E.g. : 出掛けようとしているところに、電話がかかってきた。<br/> Just when I was about to go out, the phone came.<br/> 一人で寂しくテレビを見ているところへ、友達が訪ねてきた。<br/>　　　Just when I was watching the TV, my friend came to visit me.<br/> 恋人と話しているところを、先生にみられちゃった。<br/>　　　　Just when I was talking to my lover, my teacher caught an eye of us/we were spotted by the teacher.<br/><br/>６．Another meaning of 〜ところ(を)＝〜という状態なのに、（挨拶で相手に対してよく使われる）<br/> = although, despite (polite sense)<br/> <br/>E.g.: お忙しいところをわざわざおいで頂き、誠にありがとうございました。<br/> I would like to thank you sincerely for coming despite being busy.
