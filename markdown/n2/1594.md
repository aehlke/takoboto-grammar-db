# 〜やら〜やら

## Meaning

particle denoting uncertainty or a listing used for both words and clauses

such as...and (?)

Meaning credits: The General

## Examples

### Example 6157

帰国する前は飛行機のよやくをする**やら**おみやげを買う**やら**で忙しい。

きこくするまえはひこうきのよやくをするやらおみやげをかうやらでいそがしい。

en: **What with** booking an airline and buying presents for everyone, I've been really busy before going home.

Credits: sagittarius

### Example 6459

宿題**やら**レポート**やら**で、のんびりする暇もない。

しゅくだいやらレポートやらで、のんびりするひまもない。

en: **With all** the homework and reports, I don't have a free leisure time.

Credits: iritkav

### Example 6788

彼女には、宝石**やら**バック**やら**、たくさんのプレゼントを買いましたよ。

かのじょには、ほうせきやらバックやら、たくさんのプレゼントをかいましたよ。

en: I bought lots of gifts for my girlfriend **such as** jewelry **and** bags.

Credits: 赤毛

### Example 8535

料理**やら**洗濯やら掃除で彼女の母は大変忙しかった。

りょうりやらせんたくやらそうじでかのじょのはははたいへんいそがしかった。

en: Between cooking, washing and cleaning, her mother was very busy.

あついやらむしむしするやらでじゅくすいできなかった

en: Because of heat **,** humidity **...** I could not sleep well.

### Comment 5 — Socky

Apparently, 'やら' is similar to using 'や' or 'とか' but indicates less certainty, and also has other uses as well. For example:<br/>"彼のクローゼットには、帽子やスカーフはありました。"<br/>"In his closet, there were things like caps and scarves."<br/>as opposed to:<br/>"彼のクローゼットには、帽子やらスカーフはありました。"<br/>"In his closet, (I'm fairly certain... I think) there were things like caps and scarves (or something like that)."<br/><br/>Although it apparently (according to The Handbook of Japanese Grammar by Harold G. Henderson) has other usages, in this case this is how it would be used. I heard it used in the sentence:<br/>「試してみるのか？お前が言う光とやらの力を？」<br/>I take this to mean:<br/>"Are you trying this? The power of this(uncertain of meaning)light you speak of?"<br/>Apparently とやら is the same as やら. I'm not really sure, there doesn't seem to be much info on this anywhere.<br/>***EDIT: とやら actually means "and the like" and works similarly to "nado" but once again, with less certainty and more of that "something-like-that" nuance. So the previous sentence actually should translate to:<br/>"The power of this 'light' (and such/and the like/ and stuff like that) that you speak of."<br/><br/>To be honest, it sounds like "and stuff like that" would be the closest definition. The words you are listing are said with partial certainty, and like the previous commenter said, や and とか can often be translated to "and."
