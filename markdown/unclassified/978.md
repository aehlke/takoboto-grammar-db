# 挙げ句

Source: [Takoboto #978](https://takoboto.jp/bunpo/978/)  
Label: ageku  
JLPT: unclassified  
Credits: dc

Other forms: あげく

## Meaning

in the end, after all, ended with bad results

he ended up in jail

Meaning credits: dc

## Formation

- Verb た-form + あげく + (に)

## See also

- [〜末に](../n2/824.md)

en: After talking with both (his) parents **the end result** was that I decided to divorce him.

### Comment 5 — khyron

According to a native speaker, this grammar adds a negative feeling to the sentence, so either the first part preceding the 挙句 or the following part should have a negative feel to reflect that. <br/><br/>In the example:<br/>「散々考えた挙げ句その計画を実行にうつした」<br/>this is equivalent to <br/>「散々悩んだ挙げ句その計画を実行にうつした」<br/>in feeling due to the presence of 挙句. <br/><br/>Similarly in another example<br/>「両親に相談したあげくに、彼と結婚することにした。」<br/>sounds unnatural, because the decision to marry someone is a positive one. If it was instead <br/>「両親に相談したあげくに、彼と離婚することにした。」<br/>then it would sound more natural because divorce has a negative connotation.

### Comment 21 — bamboo4

The use of 折角 in せっかく勉強したので is archaic. Nowadays, people would say 一生懸命 or 頑張って勉強したので. In modern usage, 折角 is used in negative sense, such as 折角勉強したのに落第した (Though or despite the fact that I studied hard, I failed to make the grade)
