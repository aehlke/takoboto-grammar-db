## Meaning

If I had to say...

on balance

Meaning credits: bamboo4

## Examples

### Example 4924

**どちらかと言えば**海に行きたい。

どちらかといえばうみにいきたい。

en: **If I had my druthers,** I would like to go to the sea.

Credits: bamboo4

### Example 4925

**どちらかといえば**泳ぎはうまい方でした。

どちらかといえばおよぎはうまいほうでした。

en: He was, **if anything,** a good swimmer.

Credits: bamboo4

### Example 4926

**どちらかといえば**今日はあの人に会いたくない。

どちらかといえばきょうはあのひとにあいたくない。

en: I would **rather** not see him(her) today.

Credits: bamboo4

### Comment 3 — ドニー

I suppose you could say, "If I had to say (one way or the other)" or "If I were to say..."<br/><br/>As in, one way or the other.<br/><br/>どちらかと言えば、彼女が作ったケーキはちょっと・・・<br/>If I say which way is, she-made cake is a little...<br/><br/>"If I had to say one way or the other, the cake she made is a little..."<br/><br/>I think we use this sort of construction in English, too. We say, "If I had to say..." and then state an opinion or observation.<br/><br/>In the case of stating one's desire (If I had my druthers, I'd like to go to the beach) maybe we would say "If I may say..." or something of this nature.<br/><br/>Just my two cents.
