## Meaning

(just) because of

only because he was a scholar, he could understand

Meaning credits: dc

## Discussion

### Comment 1 — dc

[dakara] is a connector meaning because. [koso] adds emphasis.<br/><br/>科学者だから、分かりました<br/>He is a scholar, so he understood<br/><br/>科学者だからこそ、分かりました<br/>(only because) he is a scholar, he was able to understand

### Comment 2 — srobertson

Note that [dakarakoso] is not usually (cannot be?) used to give a negative emphasis. You would not say that you *didn't* do something especially because of a particular reason. <br/><br/>(X) 勉強しなかったからこそ、試験に落ちたんだよ。

### Comment 3 — srobertson

I think the english explanation for this grammar point should be "especially because of..." to avoid confusion with [arebakoso], which has a stronger sense of highlighting an exclusive reason.

### Comment 4 — srobertson

One other thing - I think this entry should be changed to "karakoso", since the "da" copula is used only connect karakoso with a noun or noun adjective (or to refer back to a previous sentence, as in examples [5147], [5148] and [5149]). You can use karakoso after verbs and い adjectives as well.

### Comment 5 — winterkoninkje

I think the important thing is that it's a direct form, whether perfective or imperfective (and whether positive or negative, as I recall) plus から. As opposed to a gerund form which gives the meaning of a sequence of events (AてからB, " after A, B (happened)") instead of giving causality.<br/><br/>As for the こそ part, doesn't that have it's own page yet? Seems like it'd make sense to separate that from the rest of the pattern, or just make a note about "if using こそ it follows the から"
