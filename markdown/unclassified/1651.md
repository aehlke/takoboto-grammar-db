### Comment 1 — tigert

I`m not really sure if the translation for this is best represented above, but I know it is used in sentances that have "all you can" in them.<br/>its constructed by taking the ます　stem of the verb and adding 放題 and then it is treated as a noun. <br/><br/>I have only heard it used with verbs and I have been corrected the few times I tried to use it with する　verbs.<br/><br/>if your using a する verb, do not remove the する<br/>IE　勉強する<br/><br/>WRONG &gt;&gt;&gt;　勉強放題　<br/>RIGHT &gt;&gt;&gt; 勉強し放題<br/><br/>also I think this is a good place to mention that Japanese don`t really use this construction, except with a few select words. Although 勉強し放題 is grammaticly correct. Its 変な日本語/Strange Japanese.
