## Meaning

while doing something and taking advantage of that opportunity to do something else

getting one thing done while doing something else

ja: 〜のついでに、その機会を利用してあることをする

Meaning credits: dc

## Formation

- Noun + がてら
- Verb ます stem + がてら

### Comment 3 — Miki

gatera can be reaplced with [tsuideni]ついでに or wo[kanete] を兼ねて.<br/>○散歩ついでに、本屋に寄った。<br/>○散歩を兼ねて、本屋に寄った。<br/>ｘ散歩をしながら、本屋に寄った。This is not natural.<br/>I think that がてら is used to show another action （本屋に寄る） taken place in the middle of one main action （散歩をする）, 散歩→本屋→散歩<br/>While ながら、two or more actions are at the same time. <br/>eg. 友だちと喋りながら歩く。 I walk as talking with a friend. 歩く=喋る

### Comment 11 — Iso

がてら has two meanings. <br/>1.verb + がてら + A<br/> In this case, がてら is used to show another action(A) taken place in the middle of one main action. This can be replaced with ついでに, but not with 兼ねて.<br/><br/>2. noun(A) + がてら + B<br/> In this case, it can be replaced with ついでに or 兼ねて. But be carefull, there's a subtle difference between the two. <br/>ついでに = doing B while doing A<br/>兼ねて = doing B with the intention of doing A

### Comment 12 — mc

In the book くらべてわかる中級日本語阜ｻ文型ドリル, they talk about the fact that it depends on the location where the 2 actions take place.<br/>The 2 actions take place in same or different location: AついでにB<br/>The 2 actions take place in different location: AがてらB(unless the main action implies a movement or displacement 〜しに行く)<br/>Also the 2nd meaning of AがてらB could be "doing B will help to do A" and also only ついでに can be used at the beginning of a sentence.<br/>But the two expressions can mean "killing 2 birds with one stone".<br/><br/>In fact, after reading many exemple sentences, I think that the difference between がてら and ついでに<br/>doesn't lie in the meaning (beside the 2nd meaning of がてら).<br/>Most of the time they mean the same. What makes me choose one over the other is how I construct the sentence, which words I use and how the whole sentence sounds.<br/>In the end, I believe one shouldn't try too hard to build a rule to differentiate them.<br/>If a rule is not obvious enough or contains too much exceptions, then it shouldn't be used as a rule in the first place.<br/>I think it's one of those cases where you have 2 ways for saying the same thing, which is great so when you talk, you don't sound all the same.
