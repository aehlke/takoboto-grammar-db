# と思いきや

Source: [Takoboto #1692](https://takoboto.jp/bunpo/1692/)  
Label: toomoikiya  
JLPT: N1  
Credits: Sasato  
Retrieved: 2026-10-03T05:44:23+00:00  
License: [CC BY-SA 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.0/)

Other forms: とおもいきや

## Meaning

"thought so, but" OR, "would have thought so/imagined, but didn't"

I thought this restaurant would be cheap but (surprisingly) the bill was over 5,000 yen.

ja: 想像したろうか、いや、しなかった。思ったろうか、いや、思わなかった。

Meaning credits: Sasato

## Examples

### Example 7128

昼間だから絶対込んでいる**と思いきや**、一人もいなかった。

ひるまだからぜったいこんでいるとおもいきや、いちにんもいなかった。

en: **Despite having thought** that it must be crowded since it was afternoon, (surprisingly) not a single person was there

Credits: Sasato

### Example 7129

このレストランは安い**と思いきや**、会計は5千円以上だった！

このレストランはやすいとおもいきや、かいけいはせんえんいじょうだった！

en: **I thought** this restaurant would be cheap but (surprisingly) the bill was over 5,000 yen\!

Credits: Sasato

### Example 7896

雪国で育った彼のことだから、さぞかしスキーがうまいだろう**と思いきや**、滑っては転び、滑っては転びの繰り返しだった

ゆきぐにでそだったかれのことだから、さぞかしスキーがうまいだろうとおもいきや、すべってはころび、すべってはころびのくりかえしだった

en: For someone brought up in a snowy country, **you'd think** he'd be great at skiing, **but** he just slips and falls, slips and falls.

Credits: LR

### Example 7897

老後は退職金の蓄えで安泰だ**と思いきや**、インフレで預金が目減りしてしまった。

ろうごはたいしょくきんのたくわえであんたいだとおもいきや、インフレでよきんがめべりしてしまった。

en: In my remaining years **I thought I could** live in peace and security from the stores of my retirement savings, **but to my surprise** , inflation has diminished the value of my savings.

Credits: LR

### Example 7898

A チームの勝利で試合終了か**と思いきや**、最後に大逆転が起こった。

チームのしょうりでしあいしゅうりょうかとおもいきや、さいごにだいぎゃくてんがおこった。

en: **I thought that maybe** the tournament would end with a win for team A, but surprisingly, it ended in a huge turn in the opposite direction.

Credits: LR

## Discussion

### Comment 1 — Sasato

This is a grammar I learned out of a book and was surprised to actually hear it used in real life on a number of occasions. You use this grammar when you think one thing, but much to your astonishment, things actually turn out to be very different. You use it in the same way as you would express any thoughts, by using the quotation 「と」 and 「思う」. The only difference is that you use 「思いきや」 instead of 「思う」. There is no tense in 「思いきや」, or rather, since the results have already went against your expectations, the original thought is implicitely understood to be past tense. (from Taekim)

### Comment 3 — Tosiaki

き is a particle indicating past tense, and や is a particle meaning "but" "however" etc. This is a leftover from older Japanese, since both are no longer commonly used in modern Japanese except in this particular phrase.
