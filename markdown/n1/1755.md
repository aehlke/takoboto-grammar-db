# とばかりに

## Meaning

as if; as if ~ were convinced that; as if ~ believed that; as if ~ decided that

Takemoto vented his long held complaints as if he were convinced this was the time to do so

Meaning credits: LR

## Examples

### Example 7997

課長は余計な質問はするな**とばかりに**私ににらみつけた。

かちょうはよけいなしつもんはするなとばかりにわたしににらみつけた。

en: My section chief glared at me as if to tell me not to ask any unnecessary questions.

Credits: LR

### Example 7998

霧が晴れたので私がチャンス**とばかりに**写真を撮りまくった。

きりがはれたのでわたしがチャンスとばかりにしゃしんをとりまくった。

en: The fog had cleared, so I took pictures furiously, as if it were my only chance.

Credits: LR

### Example 7999

竹本はこの時**とばかりに**日頃の不満を吐き出した。

たけもとはこのときとばかりにひごろのふまんをはきだした。

en: Takemoto vented his long-held complaints as if he were convinced this was the time to do so.

Credits: LR

### Example 8000

彼女が舞台に登場すると、「待ってました！」**とばかりに**大きな拍手が起こった

かのじょがぶたいにとうじょうすると、「まってました！」とばかりにおおきなはくしゅがおこった

en: she appeared on the stage, and as if everyone had been waiting, huge applause broke out

Credits: LR

### Example 8001

彼女は、信じられない**とばかりに**、口を開けたまま彼を見つめていた。

かのじょは、しんじられないとばかりに、くちをあけたままかれをみつめていた。

en: She just stared at him with her mouth wide open as if she didn't believe him.

Credits: LR

## Discussion

### Comment 1 — LR

<b>BE CAREFUL</b> not to confuse ばかりに with とばかりに.<br/><strong>ばかりに</strong> is for indicating a single factor that causes a negative situation and is formed <br/>{VERB-past-tense / いadj. / なadj.stem / NOUN} +ばかりに<br/><strong>とばかりに</strong> indicates that s.o does s.t in such a way that s.o. is convinced that something is the case - "思い立ったが吉日とばかりにエアロビクスを始めました。<br/>I began aerobics believing that the day you think of something is an auspicious day to start it." and is formed<br/>{direct quotation / indirect quotation / noun} + とばかりに<br/>See [bakarini]

### Comment 4 — LR

<b>On the difference between <strong>とばかりに</strong> and <strong>と言わんばかりに</strong></b><br/><br/>と言わんばかりに"as if to say", and というように"in such a way that, as if to say" are synonymous with とばかりに and can be used in place of とばかりに in some cases.<br/><br/>However, you can't say と言わんばかりabout yourself: "The fog finally cleared so I ran outside as if to say this is my only chance" would be weird in Japanese (surely you don't need to guess your own thinking), but in this sentence you could use とばかりに and it would be more like "The fog finally cleared so I ran outside as if it was my only chance"<br/>see [toiwanbakarini]

### Comment 5 — reeceepeacee

According to Shinkanzen Master (N1), you can't use とばかりに to talk about yourself:<br/>ほかの人の様子を言う場合に使い、話者自信のことには使わない。
