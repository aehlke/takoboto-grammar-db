## Meaning

1. started, but not finished; 2. nearly occurred (but didn't)

1. the half-drunk thing in the fridge; 2. nearly hit by a car

Meaning credits: dc, Chad M.

## Discussion

### Comment 1 — dc

&lt;b&gt;~kakeru&lt;/b&gt; is for verbs:<br/>言いかけ　（いいかけ）<br/>In the middle of saying something<br/><br/>本を読みかけて、寝てしまいました<br/>I fell asleep with the book half read.<br/><br/>&lt;b&gt;~kakeno&lt;/b&gt; is a modifier for nouns (eg. tabekake no ringo)<br/>食べかけのりんご<br/>A half-eaten apple<br/><br/>&lt;b&gt;~kake da&lt;/b&gt; comes at the end of a sentence<br/>where だ is derived from です<br/>書きかけだ<br/>it is in the middle of being written

### Comment 2 — your name

食べかけの is simply the noun form of 食べかける with の acting as a modifier. I think there is no difference in meaning between '食べかけたケーキ' and '食べかけのケーキ' (although the latter is much more common).

### Comment 3 — dc

as someone else pointed out the かけた in 話しかけた　is nothing to do with this grammar, it's a set phrase <br/>彼は私に話しかけた。<br/>"he started to talk to me"
