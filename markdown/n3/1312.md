## Meaning

to do something for someone else

I will read this to you

Meaning credits: dc

## Formation

- Verb て-form + あげる

## Discussion

### Comment 1 — Furan

When someone is considering offering their services, they can use [ageru], which is usually the translation of "to give" (transitive verb here).<br/><br/>See the examples below.<br/><br/>Most of the time, people use it combined with the [-て] form. <br/><br/>Because of the nuance it adds, it's a one-way verb to use : this form is meant to humbly express yourself to someone. <br/><br/>The honorific way could be like this : <br/>貴方の為なら、何でもいたします。<br/>(Anata no tame nara, nan de mo itashimasu.)<br/>I would do anything for you.<br/>[liter. "if it is for you, I shall do anything"]<br/><br/> (it's a very basic note ; please check [kureru] and [morau] to get a better understanding of the usage of these verbs)

### Comment 2 — Nick

#566<br/>Add "。" at the end of the sentence.<br/><br/>#3727<br/>No error, but there's no need to say "あなたがたの."<br/><br/>#3728<br/>In this example, I would say "あげられる" instead of "あげる."<br/><br/>#3729<br/>No glaring error, but...<br/>あなたに代わって、買い物をして、家を掃除して、夕食を作ってあげる。<br/>This must be easier to read. <br/><br/>#3730<br/>I would say "そのうち" instead of "いつかそのうちに."<br/><br/>The other examples sound good.
