## Meaning

Vte (Continuing states, experience, repetative events)/ doing currently

The window is open.

### Comment 2 — Amatuka

Formed from -te form verb + iru (e.g. 行く → 行っている)<br/><br/>Vte = -te form verb.

### Comment 3 — Amatuka

Dropping the い [i] is a common abbreviation.<br/>(e.g. 行っている -&gt; 行ってる [itteiru → itteru])

### Comment 4 — Amatuka

There are quite a few differrent usages of this form,<br/>1. Ongoing action<br/>公園で子どもが遊んでいる。 [kouen de kodomo ga asondeiru.]<br/>(The) children are playing at the park.<br/>2. Ongoing state (result of an action)<br/>エアコンがついている。[eakon ga tsuiteiru.]<br/>The airconditioner is on.<br/>3. (as negative) Action not yet taken.<br/>まだ昼ご飯を食べていない。[mada hirugohan wo tabeteinai.]<br/>I haven't had lunch yet.<br/>(Note this is different from 昼ご飯を食べない。[hirugohan wo tabenai.] I'm not going to eat lunch.<br/>4. Experience<br/>本を書いてる。[hon wo kaiteru.] I've written a book.<br/>5. Repetative actions.<br/>僕はよく映画館に行っている。[boku ha yoku eigakan ni itteiru.]<br/>I often go to the cinema.<br/>6. Condition<br/>彼は太っている。[kare ha futotteiru.]<br/>He's fat.

### Comment 5 — Amatuka

ている [teiru] can't be used with the verbs of existance (e.g. ある [aru] and いる [iru])

### Comment 6 — tataku

2. Ongoing state (result of an action)<br/> エアコンがついている。[eakon ga tsuiteiru.]<br/> The airconditioner is on.<br/>Note that this use is only with intransitive verbs. If it were the transitive form of this verb (tsukeru, rather than tsuku), it would be -tearu (eakon ga tsuketearu).

### Comment 10 — tigert

Hey ミサ, I asked my coworker what the diffrence is and he confirmed your guess. <br/><br/>待って　is something that is currently being done and 待ってて is something that will be started after the completion of something else. <br/><br/>IE if your teacher askes for your homework and you have to dig in your backpack to get it, 待ってください<br/><br/>if you are in the middle of completing your homework. 待っててください。<br/><br/>I give you 10 points for posting this question. I have always used both interchangable (not a problem if you do) but its always good to learn a little more about the smaller meanings within the words.
