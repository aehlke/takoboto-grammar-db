## Discussion

### Comment 1 — dc

this is also used for soft suggetsions to do something.<br/>タバコを消せばいい if you could put out your cigarette

### Comment 2 — Amatuka

ichidan (regular group 2) verbs replace -ru with -reba たべる (taberu) → 食べれば (tabereba).<br/>godan (regular group 1) verbs change last kana to -e column and add -ba. 行く (iku) → 行けば (ikeba)

### Comment 3 — Amatuka

The following is not good Japanese - えば can't be used in sentences of the form "If X then please do Y."<br/><br/>(BAD) 彼がそちらに着けば、知らせてください。<br/>If he arrives there, let him know.<br/><br/>Instead -tara can be used.<br/><br/>(GOOD) 彼がそちらに着いたら、知らせてください。<br/>When he arrives there, let him know.

### Comment 4 — Amatuka

えば can be used if the following sentence is expressed as a likely outcome.<br/>(GOOD) 彼がそちらに着けば、知らせてくれるでしょう。<br/>I guess you'll let me know if he turns up there.

### Comment 7 — dc

### Comment 8 — Mukade

Actually, the command form is okay as long as the えば is not a verb.<br/>(BAD) 田中さんが着ければ、教えてください。<br/>(GOOD) よろしければ、食べてください。

### Comment 9 — horms

I think that the English in ex #4977 is a little strange:<br/><br/>走れば、すぐに彼に追いつくだろう。 <br/>You will soon come up with him if you run. <br/><br/>I think that either of the following better reflects the Japanese text.<br/><br/>You will soon catch up to/with him if you run.<br/>You will soon overtake/pass him if your run.<br/><br/>Also, in ex #4980, I think leave would be better than start.<br/><br/>すぐに出発すれば電車に間に合うだろう。 <br/>You will be in time for the train if you leave at once. <br/><br/>Depart could also be used in place of start (or leave), though it sounds a little misleading as there is a strong connection between trains and departing.

### Comment 10 — dante

Maybe in ex #6441 ーたら　form is more preferable? 「この薬を飲んだらよくなります。」<br/><br/>Some more examples:<br/>秋が来れば雨が降る。(Expresses usual occasion. In this case 'と' may be used, but not 'ーたら')<br/>誰か寄ってくれば赤ちゃんはたいてい泣き始める。(This one expresses usual occasion too. As in above example えば may be replaced with 'と', but not with ーたら)<br/><br/>新しくなければ買いません。 [If it is not new, I will not buy it.] (Expresses intention)<br/>怖ければ後ろに隠れたほうがいい。[If it is scary, you'd better hide behind.] (This is not a command, but in this example なら would be better, I think).

### Comment 11 — kinoko

Okay, let me see if I've got this straight...<br/><br/>「えば」 and 「たら」 are similar, but 「えば」 is used to express more of a cause-and-effect sort of relationship, and that's why it's used to express natural consequences. 「たら」 has less of a cause-and-effect tone, and it implies "when" or "after" more than 「えば」.
