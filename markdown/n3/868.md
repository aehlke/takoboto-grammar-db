## Meaning

by the way

by the way it looks like rain

ja: それはそうと。話題を変えるときに使う語。

Meaning credits: dc, Uniheld

## Examples

### Example 1099

雨が降ってきたなあ**ところで**飯にしないか

あめがふってきたなあところでめしにしないか

en: Hey, it's starting to rain **By the way** you want to go get something to eat?

Credits: bamboo4

### Example 1101

( 「ところで」の他の意味 )私が説得した**ところで**あいつは耳をかさないだろう.

「ところで」のほかのいみわたしがせっとくしたところであいつはみみをかさないだろう

en: (alternate usage) **No matter how much** I try to persuade him, that guy won't listen.

Credits: dc

### Example 4942

**ところで**、あなたの仕事は何ですか。

ところで、あなたのしごとはなにですか。

en: **By the way,** what do you do?

## Discussion

### Comment 1 — IMABI

Tokoro de is normally written in Hiragana although Kanji is possible. It will always be used to change the topic in a conversation. So if you were to see, "By the way...." it could start a clause or be after a complete sentence where you or someone says something and you use tokoro de to add more information or something that popped into your mind because of it.

### Comment 2 — dc

different ways of saying "by the way"<br/><br/>chinami ni &gt; related to that...<br/>tokorode &gt; by the way (unrelated)<br/>soieba &gt; speaking of that<br/><br/>from here<br/><br/>「ところで」は、今まで話していた事とまったく違う話題に変えるとき使います。「ちなみに」は、今まで話していたことと関連して他の例などを挙げるときや、ある話題に対して相手の感想などを聞くときにも使います。だから「ところで」と「ちなみに」はまったく違う意味を持っています。

### Comment 13 — Lurchig

ところで in its purely grammatical function is nowadays written almost exclusively in hiragana to differentiate it from its many other uses. JPDB has ところで at 99% and 所で at less than 1%, which seems about right. Either way, 処で is never used nowadays. I would be okay with leaving 所で in here for completeness' sake but question how useful it is since this is a guide for beginners. Either way, 処で should be removed.
