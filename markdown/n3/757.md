## Meaning

Vm, wanting to do \_\_\_

I want to see a Bond movie\!

Meaning credits: Amatuka

## Examples

### Example 711

ボンドの映画を見**たい**な〜。

ボンドのえいがをみたいな

en: I **wanna** see the Bond movie\!

Credits: Amatuka

### Example 5242

この車を**買いたい**んですが、お金が無い。

このくるまをかいたいんですが、おかねがない。

en: I **want to buy** this car but I don't have the money.

Credits: eastmael

### Example 6529

あなたは何をし**たい**のですか。

あなたはなにをしたいのですか。

en: What would you like to do?

Credits: Saralynne

### Example 8164

彼女は私のすることをいつみ知りたがる。

かのじょはわたしのすることをいつみしりたがる。

en: She\`s always curious about (as to) what I\`m doing.

## Discussion

### Comment 1 — Amatuka

Note that when speaking of what somebody else wants you should use the -がる form. E.g. たい→たがる. Basically you shouldn't say "She wants to ___" but "She looks like she wants to ___".<br/><br/>In asking questions the direct 〜たい form is fine.

### Comment 2 — Amatuka

In modern Japanese 〜たい generally takes を with が being used for emphasis when strong feelings are involved.

### Comment 3 — Amatuka

### Comment 9 — thekaje

Mathrick, the sentence you've provided is a quotation, as indicated by the quote-marker って (って is a common abbreviation of と). Therefore, in your example sentence, it is appropriate to use the first-person form of 欲しい, because the speaker is quoting/paraphrasing another's first-person remark.
