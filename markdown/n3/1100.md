# たり する　form

Source: [Takoboto #1100](https://takoboto.jp/bunpo/1100/)

Credits: dc, Savta

Other forms: V stem+たり

## Meaning

doing things like V+ing

Yesterday I did things like watching tv and playing video games.

ja: Vたり、　Vたり　します。

Meaning credits: dc, Savta

## Discussion

### Comment 1 — dc

The [-tari] form is used when a sequence of verbs occur in a row. The last one cannot be a tari, it needs to conclude the sentence.<br/><br/>normal:<br/>　食べたり、飲んだり、のんびりしました。<br/>　Eating and drinking, I relaxed.<br/><br/>neg:<br/>　読まなかったり、行きたくなかったり、何もしなかったり、悪い人でした。<br/>　not reading, not going, not doing anything, he was a bad guy.<br/><br/>with adjectives:<br/>　サービスは良かったり、値段安かったり、、いいんじゃない？<br/>　good service, low prices... alright eh?<br/><br/>neg with adjectives:<br/>　サービスが悪かったり、値段が高くなかったり、、、<br/> the service is not bad, the prices aren't high...

### Comment 2 — Ayumi

Is also used fo repeated actions, like:<br/>Watashi wa nomitari tabetari shimasu.<br/>(I drank and ate repeatedly)<br/>You must add "suru/shimasu" at the end.

### Comment 3 — tigert

I changed this grammar note a little. [tari suru]<br/>form means "doing things like". the Verb takes the plain past tense form IE 食べる become 食べた and then you add the り<br/>買う will become 買った　then you add the り,etc...<br/><br/>きのう、私は テレビを見たり、ゲームをしたり しました。

### Comment 4 — infinite\_trial

-し is usually used for phrases that describes your subject. for example you can say that you like a certain singer because he has a good voice, nice attitude and he is handsome. you end the sentence with a conclusion that you like the singer, and enumerate the reasons by using し.
