# と言うと

Source: [Takoboto #1145](https://takoboto.jp/bunpo/1145/)  
Label: toiuto  
JLPT: N3  
Credits: dc

Other forms: というと

## Meaning

when you talk of, speaking of, when you say

talking of the french...

Meaning credits: dc

## See also

- [〜と](../n3/1368.md)

## Examples

### Example 378

スイス**というと**何をイメージしますか？

スイスというとなにをイメージしますか？

en: **When you hear** 'Switzerland' what does it invoke in your mind?

Credits: Amatuka, bamboo4

### Example 3109

なぜクビにならなかったか**と言うと**彼には超能力があり、彼の予知で数々の難事件が解決をみたからだ

なぜクビにならなかったかというとかれにはちょうのうりょくがあり、かれのよちでかずかずのなんじけんがかいけつをみたからだ

en: **To say (the reason)** why he wasn't fired, having psychic powers, he solved many difficult cases with his foresight.

Credits: bi-ru, bamboo4

### Example 3115

何かと**いうと**あいつはいつも口を出そうとする。

なにかというとあいつはいつもくちをだそうとする。

en: Whatever **happens,** that guy always tries to interject himself.

Credits: bamboo4, kparam

### Example 5945

何故か**といわれても**、知りません

なぜかといわれても、しりません

en: **If** you ask me why, I don't know.

Credits: swing39

### Comment 11 — nhk9

I don't really know what the meanings for both 何か というとnor the meaning for 何か_と_いったら. However, I think there's a slight difference between toiuto and toittara. Both means about "speaking of..." but the former is just stating something in a neutral sense, whereas the latter one is stating something more of surprise, or beauty.<br/><br/>So I think it's more common to say this "沖縄といったら、文字でかけないほど美しい浜辺や海がたくさんあるようだね" with toittara than with toiuto. I found this in the Kanzen Masuta textbook for level 2 grammar.

### Comment 12 — bamboo4

といったら is used when making a conclusive or definitive statement, allowing nothing else, whereas というと is more generic and allows many alternatives.<br/>Compare 日本の山といったら富士山だ(when you say which is THE mountain in Japan, it would have to be Mt. Fuji) with the example #378 above.

### Comment 13 — LittleFish

I've heard "何故と言ったら" before in a few lectures. The speaker used "なぜと言ったら..." to push the lecture in a further direction, (seeing as there was no audience participation to tell him to keep going), to keep it flowing. Almost as if to say " "Why?" you may ask; well, blah blah blah."
