## Meaning

(\[verb\]-te, connects two sentences)

I'm going to the book shop to buy it.

Meaning credits: Amatuka

## Examples

### Example 3118

茶碗を口のところまで持って**きて**、箸でごはんをかきこむのは行儀が悪い。

ちゃわんをくちのところまでもってきて、はしでごはんをかきこむのはぎょうぎがわるい。

en: **Bringing**  the bowl up to your mouth and shoveling rice in with chopsticks is bad manners.

Credits: KotatsuSama

### Example 707

この赤く**て**大きい車は田中さんのです。

このあかくておおきいくるまはたなかさんのです。

en: This large **and** red car is Mr Tanaka's.

Credits: Amatuka

### Example 888

本屋に**行って**買います。

ほんやにいってかいます。

en: I'm going to the book shop **and** buying it.

Credits: Amatuka

### Example 6248

メロンを食べ**て**、コーヒーを飲みました。

メロンをたべて、コーヒーをのみました。

en: He ate some melon and drank some coffee.

Credits: fio

### Example 6249

歩い**て**来ます。

あるいてきます。

en: I come on foot (walking).

Credits: fio

### Example 6250

妹は遊んでいます。

いもうとはあそんでいます。

en: My younger sister is playing (right now).

Credits: fio

### Example 6251

社長はたいへん怒っ**て**います。

しゃちょうはたいへんおこっています。

en: The director is very angry (he became angry and is still angry).

Credits: fio

### Example 6252

喜んで手伝います。

よろこんでてつだいます。

en: It'll be a pleasure to help you out.

Credits: fio

### Example 6253

手紙をもっ**て**行きました。

てがみをもっていきました。

en: I brought the letter (lit. "having the letter, I came").

Credits: fio

### Example 6254

田中さんは出掛け**て**います。

たなかさんはでかけています。

en: Mr. Tanaka's gone out (Mr. Tanaka is not here).

Credits: fio

## Discussion

### Comment 1 — KotatsuSama

te is more of a "parallel" tense in Japanese. It reflects that the verb goes on along with the rest of the sentence or in a sequence.<br/>te-iru is the same way, the verb is going on at the same time "you exist" hence the -ing.

### Comment 2 — fio

The -te form has many uses; here there are just some of them:<br/><br/>1) It's a way to connect two sentences (a kind of equivalent expression for "and").<br/><br/>2) Equivalent to English "-ing".<br/><br/>3) -te+iru: to describe an action that is now happening or that is not already finished.<br/>[There are similar grammatical structures with verbs like "shimau", "ageru", "yaru", "miru"... but I guess they should have their own section]<br/><br/>4) Replacing an adverb.<br/><br/>5) Motte+iku/kuru: to bring, to take, to carry something.<br/><br/>6) Present perfect in some cases.<br/><br/>When the -te form is applied to adjectives finished in -i, it has also a copulative function: joining two or more adjectives by adding -kute instead -i. <br/><br/>天気が悪くて、たいへん困りました。<br/>The weather was bad, and that bothered us.<br/><br/>あの映画は短く面白いです。= あの映画は短くて面白いです。<br/>That film is short and amusing.<br/><br/>[I must apologize for the possible mistakes this note may have. English is not my native language]

### Comment 5 — Amatuka

Also -i adjectives join with -kute -くて<br/>The copula has the 'te' form of で.

### Comment 6 — Amatuka

Verbs with auxillary verbs and auxillary adjectives use the -te form. e.g. やってみてください。<br/>やる(in -te form, to do) みる (in -te form, to try) ください (request). All together Please try to do it.
