## Examples

### Example 471

面接に行く**から**はげましてください

めんせつにいくからはげましてください

en: I'm going to an interview **so** please encourage me.

Credits: Amatuka

### Example 3051

必要**から**発明が生まれる。

ひつようからはつめいがうまれる。

en: Inventions are made **as dictated by** necessity.

Credits: bamboo4

### Example 4831

あの日帰ったのは体調が悪かった**から**です。

あのひかえったのはたいちょうがわるかったからです。

en: It is **because** the physical condition was bad that I returned that day.

Credits: Miki

### Example 4832

ああ、ゆっくりやって下さい。急ぎません**から。**

ああ、ゆっくりやってください。いそぎませんから。

en: Oh, take your time. **(Because)** I'm in no hurry.

Credits: Miki

### Example 8667

昨夜病気だ**から**、眠れなかった。

さくやびょうきだから、ねむれなかった。

en: **Because** I was sick, I couldn't sleep last night.

Credits: 反戦

### Comment 6 — yookoso

I saw the following explanation of the difference between kara and node on a mailing list today: <br/><br/>The conjunction "kara" and "node" both express reason or cause. While "kara" is used for reason or cause of a speaker's volition, opinion and so on, "node" is for existing (existed) action or situation.<br/>- Kino wa samukatta node uchi ni imashita. Since it was cold, I stayed home.<br/>- Atama ga itakatta node gakkou o yasunda. Since I had a headache, I didn't go to school. <br/>- Totemo shizukadatta node yoku nemuremashita. Since it was very quiet, I could sleep well.<br/>- Yoku benkyou shita node shiken ni goukaku shita. Since I studied hard, I passed the examination.<br/><br/>The sentences expressing personal judgment such as speculation, suggestion, intention, request, opinion, volition, invitation, and so forth must take "kara."<br/>- Kono kawa wa kitanai kara tabun sakana wa inai deshou. Since this river is polluted, there is probably no fish. <br/>- Mou osoi kara hayaku nenasai. Go to bed, since it is getting late.<br/>- Kono hon wa totemo omoshiroi kara yonda hou ga ii. This book is very interesting, so you'd better to read it.<br/>- Kono kuruma wa furui kara atarashi kuruma ga hoshii desu. This car is old, so I want a new car.<br/><br/>While "kara" focuses more on the reason, "node" focuses more on the resulting effect. This is why the "kara" clause is used independently more often than "node".<br/>- Doushite okureta no. Why were you late?<br/>- Densha ni nori okureta kara. Because I missed the train.<br/><br/>"Kara" can be immediately followed by "~ desu."<br/>- Atama ga itakatta kara desu. Because I had a headache. (OK)<br/>- Atama ga itakatta node desu. Because I had a headache. (Not OK = Wrong)<br/><br/>Any comments, corrections?
