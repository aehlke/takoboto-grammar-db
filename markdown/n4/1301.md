# 〜始める

Source: [Takoboto #1301](https://takoboto.jp/bunpo/1301/)  
Label: -hajimeru  
JLPT: N4  
Credits: dc  
Retrieved: 2026-10-03T05:32:22+00:00  
License: [CC BY-SA 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.0/)

Other forms: 〜はじめる

## Meaning

to begin/start VERB-ing

I started reading yesterday.

ja: ある活動を始める

Meaning credits: dc

## Formation

- Verb stem + 始める

## Examples

### Example 4365

６時を過ぎると従業員は帰り**始めた**。

６じをすぎるとじゅうぎょういんはかえりはじめた。

en: After 6 p.m. the employees **began to**  return home.

Credits: Miki

### Example 1180

本を読み**始めた**

en: I really **began** reading books in earnest once I was a University Student.

## Discussion

### Comment 1 — KyleGoetz

This construct expresses the beginning of an action or verb. It takes the [conjunctive form] of the verb and appends to it the auxiliary verb 始める.

### Comment 2 — Sakuratree

Hajimaru which also means to start same as hajimeru means the same.<br/><br/>However, hajimaru doesn't require someone to do something and it never takes the を particle while hajimeru needs を.<br/><br/>Japanese contains these types of verbs: transitive and intransitive.

### Comment 3 — arkofnoah

出す have a nuance of sudden action<br/><br/>走り出す means to "break into a run", like when a girl sees a cockroach and she "走り出す"。<br/><br/>始めた have a more neutral meaning, as in started eating, or start running in the case of "走り始めた" (like you start running when a race start.)<br/><br/>so that's what i think...
