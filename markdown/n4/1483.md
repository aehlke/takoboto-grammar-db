## Meaning

One's intention; conviction; belief

I intend to do that; I am convinced that ...

ja: その意志

Meaning credits: 赤毛

## Formation

- Verb dictionary form + つもり + (だ)
- Verb nai form + つもり + (だ)
- Verb dictionary form + つもり + だった
- Verb nai form + つもり + だった

## Discussion

### Comment 1 — 赤毛

Two ways to inform the interlocutor of the wishes of the speaker <br/>(different in Japanese from the way to tell about the wishes of a third person):<br/>1) Verb in volitional form (意向形) + と + 思う: <br/>jp: 一万円ほど父に都合してもらおう<strong>と思う。<br/>en: I think I will ask my father to lend me 10000 yen.<br/>2) Verb in plain form + noun to mark intention + だ or です or である<br/>The noun can be 考え（かんがえ）,予定（よてい）, or 積もり（つもり）which is the more common.<br/>This last way is easier, for example to say you don't want to do something. The negative form of つもりだ is つもりはない (and not つもりではない).<br/><br/>Be careful, the use of つもり after た形 have a different meaning <br/>(see entry [tatsumoride] and [tsumoridatta]). </strong>

### Comment 3 — Campbell

つもり does not really mean intention. American textbooks and instructors simply explain it as such because it is simpler. つもり really describes a state of mind. 行くつもりだ it is the state of mind to go = I intend to go. what about: 行ったつもりだ? in english it doesn't make sense to say: I intend to went... it is I am in the frame of mind as if I had gone... if that makes any sense...<br/><br/>よく読んだつもりです。I am convinced I read it carefully.<br/>一所懸命やったつもりで。　I believe I did my best.<br/>(Examples provided from Making Sense of Japanese: What the Textbooks don't tell you by Jay Rubin)

### Comment 5 — tigert

The thing about つもり is that using it gives the listener the impression that you won`t complete the action you intend on doing. From the people that I have talked with there are levels of commitment in the japanese language. the first level is negative statement. which is avoided at all cost.<br/>EX No, I won`t go.<br/><br/>then comes passive nagative statement.<br/><br/>EX I would like to go, but ....(I`m not going)<br/><br/>then comes probablility related statements. which have their own level.<br/>行くかも知らない I don`t know if I`ll go,(but I probably won`t)<br/>行くつもり　I intend to go (but I won`t go because well you know how it is)<br/>多分行く　I think I will go ( unless something comes up.) <br/><br/>and the final stage is the affirmative statement.<br/>行く I will go.

I'd like to explain this completely differently, if I can risk it.<br/><br/>つもり should be better described as an adverb of certainty, being VERY CERTAIN.<br/><br/>An example you won't often see, but that illustrates this could be:<br/><br/>彼女はきれいなつもりだ<br/>She is really good looking, isn't she (or, "She's hot!")<br/><br/>so,<br/> 会議には出ないつもりです <br/>= The meeting - to attend - not - is certain.
