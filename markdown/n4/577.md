# 下さい

Source: [Takoboto #577](https://takoboto.jp/bunpo/577/)  
Label: kudasai  
JLPT: N4  
Credits: Amatuka  
Retrieved: 2026-10-03T05:32:12+00:00

Other forms: ください

## Meaning

please

Please give me that

Meaning credits: Amatuka

## Formation

- て-form + ください
- を + ください

### Comment 1 — ramu

[kudasai] is an auxiliary verb which indicates a polite request: "please do"<br/><br/>1) kudasai is a polite imperative form of [kudasaru], the honorofic version of [kureru] 'give'(me) and is used as an auxiliary verb with the te-form of verbs.<br/>　ゆっくり飲んでください。<br/>　please drink slowly/at your own pace<br/><br/>2) The negtative question form [kudasaimasenka] makes a request more polite　<br/>　あした８時に来てくださいませんか。<br/>　Won't you come tomorrow at 8 O'Clock?<br/><br/>3) [douzo] emphasizes the speakers request and makes it more polite.<br/>　どうぞたくさん食べてください。<br/>　Go ahead please, and eat as much as you like<br/><br/>4) In very informal speech, kudasai may drop(this form of request is often used by female speakers)<br/>　はやく言って。<br/>　go on say it (please)<br/><br/>　まだ帰らないで。<br/>　dont come back yet (please)<br/><br/>5) [kure], the imperative form of kureru, can also be used in place of kudasai in informal male speech.[Vte/Vneg nai de] kure is the least polite request form.<br/>　いっしょに来てくれ。<br/>　can you come together (for me?)<br/> (=can you let me go with you?)<br/><br/>　うちへ帰らないでくれ。<br/> don't come back to our place (OK?)

### Comment 2 — Amatuka

Note : The object marker を is said 'o' but may be written as either 'wo' or 'o' depending on romaji system used.

### Comment 3 — Amatuka

ください can be used in two ways.<br/>1. To ask for an object をください ( wo kudasai )<br/>2. To ask for an action verb-te ください (v-te kudasai).

### Comment 4 — Amatuka

Note みせる (miseru) is the dictionary form of the verb meaning 'to show'.<br/>みせて (misete) is the te form of the verb meaning 'to show'.

### Comment 15 — KyleGoetz

I went through the examples and replaced 下 with くだ. Japanese manuals of style say to use the kanji with NOUNを下さい, contrasted with the TE-form VERBてください, in which you do not use the kanji.<br/><br/>It's not technically incorrect, but it's preferred, similar to how in English spelling out zero through ten is preferred, but using numbers for values greater than ten is preferred.
