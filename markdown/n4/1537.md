## Meaning

too; too much; excessively

This problem is too difficult for me

Meaning credits: ness

## Formation

- Verb stem + すぎる
- な-adjective (without な) + すぎる
- い-adjective (without い) + すぎる

### Comment 1 — Ness

added to Verbs: Conjunctive form of Verb <br/>(行く--&gt;行き；食べる--&gt;食べ) + すぎる<br/> add to Adjectives: Root of Adjective<br/>(むずかしい--&gt;むすかし；大きい--&gt;大き）+　すぎる<br/><br/>This construction can mean "too much," as in, <br/>むずかしすぎる (too hard); 大きすぎる (too big) or it can mean "excessively," as in, 行きすぎる (to go too much); 食べすぎる (to eat too much).

To me, "that person quickly speaks too much" doesn't make much sense. hayaku hanashisugiru means "speak too quickly" because hayaku is an adverb. Thus, it becomes hayakuhanasu + sugiru (speaks quickly + too much) = speaks too quickly

### Comment 13 — TheoMurpse

It should be noted that you don't use the kanji for 〜すぎる because it is an auxiliary verb in this construct, and one should not use kanji for auxiliary verbs. Similarly, one does not say 食べて来た nor 読んで行った, but rather 食べてきた and 読んでいった.

### Comment 17 — Morse

I edited the translation of "このりんごは甘すぎて、食べられない。" cause when you say "X すぎて Y", you're actually establishing a direct causal relation between X and Y, as in "X happens in excess and that causes Y to happen". While "This apple is too sweat to eat" technically means the same thing, it might confuse people when they find sentences that might not be possible to change the wording that way, like "It's so cold that I'm shivering"(寒すぎて、震えている。), and they might not understand that the actual meaning of the phrase about the apple is that the apple being too sweat makes me incapable of eating it, hence the 食べられない.
